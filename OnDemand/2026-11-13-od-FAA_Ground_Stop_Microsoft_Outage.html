FAA Orders Nationwide Ground Stop a Day After Microsoft 365 Outage

BLUF

Reporting through 17:23 ET establishes no link between the 13 Nov nationwide ground stop and the 12 Nov Microsoft 365 outage. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said he sees no reason to connect them. The FAA has not identified what failed, and Microsoft has not issued its final report.

Key Facts

  • The FAA ordered all U.S. departures held until further notice on the afternoon of 13 Nov. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced the stop at 4:16 p.m. ET and said FAA technicians were investigating (NBC News; BBC News).
  • Mullin said the cause was a telecommunications outage throughout the FAA, that every flight was accounted for, and that officials had no reason to believe the country faced a threat (CBS News; Fox News).
  • Reporting through 5:23 p.m. ET did not confirm that the FAA had lifted the order or give a restoration time. The agency described the cause only as a temporary telecommunications outage (CBS News; Newsweek).
  • Microsoft 365 services including Outlook failed from 12 Nov. Microsoft blamed “an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade,” said it found no malicious activity, apologized, and reported broad recovery on 13 Nov (CNBC).
  • Microsoft's incident began on 12 Nov, around 1 p.m. ET by TechRadar's account. The FAA command center posted the ground stop at 3:54 p.m. ET on 13 Nov, at least 26 hours later (TechRadar; Yahoo News).
  • Two FAA employees told the Washington Examiner the stop followed a “rolling frequency outage” at facilities that monitor aircraft in flight. The paper said it viewed documentation of the halt (Washington Examiner).
  • FlightAware listed at least 8,000 delays and 400 cancellations shortly after the order. Chicago and Midway were among the first airports to halt departures as controllers lost frequencies (CBS News; Washington Examiner).

Points of Dispute

Whether the two events are connected. Mullin said they are not (CNN). No outlet reviewed reports an official or company asserting a link. The available descriptions point to separate systems: Microsoft cites a network capacity upgrade (CNBC), while FAA employees cite a frequency outage at air traffic facilities (Washington Examiner). No outlet reviewed describes the FAA's controller radio systems as dependent on Microsoft services. Microsoft reported recovery on 13 Nov, before the FAA order. Neither agency has published a final cause. The FAA's finding and Microsoft's post-incident report will settle it.

Whether a cyberattack or the FAA's new AI system caused the stop. Mullin said there was no indication of a “nefarious attack” (ABC News), but also said the department was not ruling anything out and was examining whether the AI-supported FAA system contributed (CNN). Law enforcement officials told CNN that preliminary indications pointed away from a cyberattack. ASI, the system's developer, said the stop did not result from its SMART system or its integration with the FAA (CNN). No investigation finding is public.

How many facilities failed and where it began. Duffy said all Air Route Traffic Control Centers were affected (NBC News). The FAA's statement said multiple centers (Newsweek). Anonymous accounts differ on onset: a pilot relayed Kansas City Center (Yahoo News), and a controller cited a frequency issue at one East Coast airport at 3:36 p.m. ET (Scripps News). Accounts of the first failure point therefore remain anecdotal. The FAA has confirmed neither, and has not said whether a carrier circuit, equipment or software failed.

Background

The FAA ordered its first nationwide ground stop after the 11 Sep 2001 attacks. The second came on 11 Jan 2023, after an outage in the pilot-alert system, and lasted about 90 minutes (The Hill). On 21 Sep 2026 a failed primary circuit and a cut backup fiber line disrupted hundreds of Northeast flights. No outlet reviewed links any of those earlier failures to Microsoft or another cloud provider. AP noted similar communications outages in Minneapolis and Dallas, and said the FAA had replaced 3,346 communications lines since receiving a $12.5 billion down payment (AP, via AJC). The same day the FAA began testing an AI tool that predicts schedule conflicts and weather problems (AP, via WLOX). Microsoft's 12 Nov outage shut down Outlook and other 365 services for nearly 24 hours. The company reported recovery on 13 Nov and promised a post-incident report, with no sign of malicious activity (CNBC).

Watch

  1. FAA identifies the failed system or fault behind the 13 Nov outage and states when operations resumed, by 30 Nov. Assessment: Likely (moderate confidence), based on the FAA naming causes within a day after the 21 Sep 2026 Northeast failure and the January 2023 stop, and on DHS already investigating.
  2. Microsoft's post-incident report or the FAA's finding ties the 13 Nov stop to the Microsoft outage by 30 Nov. Assessment: Very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on Mullin's denial, the 26-hour gap, and Microsoft's attribution to its own network change.
  3. DHS or the Transportation Department reports that the FAA's new AI system or a cyber intrusion contributed to the stop by 30 Nov. Assessment: Unlikely (low confidence), based on ASI's denial, no reported indication of an attack, and reports of a frequency outage.
