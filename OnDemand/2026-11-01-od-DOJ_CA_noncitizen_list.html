DOJ's Noncitizen List in California: Contents, Method and Court Status

BLUF

On 13 Oct the Justice Department gave California officials a list of 20,000-plus registered voters it calls likely noncitizens and demanded their ballots be set aside. A federal judge denied that request on 28 Oct, the Ninth Circuit declined to intervene, and a special master will now develop the factual record on the list and on whether ballots can be separated.

Key Facts

  • LA County's Registrar says the file lists names, dates of birth and addresses. It has no unique voter identifier, carries several often conflicting birth dates, and holds each address in one cell (County filing, Dkt. 32).
  • Only 7,035 of the 20,000-plus records bear LA County addresses. In a 200-record county sample, 42.9% were canceled registrations, 4.6% inactive, and 71.2% had never voted. The county says these figures describe the records, not individuals' citizenship (Dkt. 32; NPR).
  • DOJ declarants say a California roster dated August 2025 was enhanced with third-party data, paired to alien numbers, checked against State Department and Social Security records, and reviewed by officers. USCIS later flagged about 189 listed records with data discrepancies (Dkt. 74; Newsweek).
  • On 19 Oct DOJ charged 28 foreign nationals with unlawfully registering to vote. DOJ says all 28 appear on the list of 20,000-plus names and remain registered (DOJ release; Washington Times).
  • Judge David Carter denied DOJ's request on 28 Oct, citing the National Voter Registration Act's 90-day provision. The Ninth Circuit denied an injunction pending appeal on 30 Oct, 2–1, noting the district court found no disclosed error rates (Washington Times; Washington Examiner; Ninth Circuit opinion).
  • Carter appointed special master Daniel Garrie on 29 Oct to develop the factual record (Washington Times; Ninth Circuit opinion).

Points of Dispute

Reliability. DOJ says about 800 federal employees checked each name by hand, and its affiant calls the county's sample findings false while conceding some citizens may be listed (AP). LA County says its sample shows the list is unreliable, citing canceled registrations, no voting history, and 38.9% of records with a reported birthplace showing U.S. birth (Dkt. 32). No primary source settles this; the special master is developing the factual record.

Delivery. DOJ says it made the list available to the Secretary of State on 13 Oct. Secretary Weber says DOJ never sent it to her office (NBC News). DOJ's own letter offered her a digital copy on request (Dkt. 1-3).

Authority. DOJ argues 18 U.S.C. § 611 requires California to withhold these ballots. California answers that state law and the National Voter Registration Act bar it (Weber letter).

Background

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin wrote Weber on 16 Jul that up to 190,832 registrants may be noncitizens. Weber asked for the methodology on 23 Jul and says none came. DOJ's 13 Oct letters gave her and LA County Registrar Dean Logan 48 hours to review the list and segregate, not cancel, unconfirmed ballots (Fox News; Dkt. 1-3). DOJ sued on 15 Oct (No. 2:26-cv-12025).

Watch

  1. Carter's ruling on DOJ's renewed preliminary injunction request, due after a 13 Nov evidentiary hearing. Assessment: A ruling granting the injunction is unlikely (moderate confidence), based on the 28 Oct denial and two Ninth Circuit rulings against DOJ.
  2. Garrie's findings on the list before the 11 Dec state certification deadline. Assessment: Findings before 11 Dec are likely (low confidence), because Carter said the review could finish by then; a report deadline is not available.
  3. A separate federal judge's 30 Oct order restraining DOJ from demanding that officials halt ballot processing, which expires 16 Nov (Washington Times).
