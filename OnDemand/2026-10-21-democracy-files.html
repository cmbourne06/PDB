The Democracy Files: A Primary-Source Assessment
What the recordings and documents actually show about post-election planning by a coordinated network of former U.S. officials, academics, and civil-society operators
Note on Sourcing

This report draws exclusively from primary source materials: audio recordings and their transcripts from five separate sessions between January and October 2026, and a persons-of-interest profile compiled from the same archive. Social media amplification of these materials is addressed separately in Section 6. This report does not adjudicate the framing of any secondary source; it reports what the primary sources say, in context.

Section 1

Overview and Bottom-Line Assessment

A network of former U.S. diplomats, USAID officials, retired military flag officers, academics, and civil-society operators has been conducting coordinated planning since at least January 2026 for scenarios in which a Democratic midterm sweep triggers a sustained campaign against the Trump administration — including pre-organized street mobilization, engagement with governors and mayors, international solidarity coordination, and contingency framing for events they describe as crossing constitutional "red lines." The planning is real, organized, and more operationally advanced than routine political opposition. It does not, on the evidence of these recordings, constitute a coup, a secession plan, or a CIA-directed operation.
What These Documents Show
  • A named network — operating under the Renew to Thrive (R2T), Renew American Democracy (RAD), Democracy Renewal Group, and National Security Leaders for America (NSL4A) umbrellas — has developed a three-tier structure: doctrine/research at the top, former national-security operators in the middle, and protest/legal/street execution below.
  • The network has identified three "emergency scenarios" — militarization, election meltdown, and constitutional crisis — and conducted planning sessions, breakout groups, and at least one in-person tabletop exercise in Washington, D.C. for each.
  • Planners have engaged or sought to engage governors (through "Governors Safeguarding Democracy"), mayors, Senate staff, and election officials with preparatory materials and briefings.
  • The network has explicitly solicited input from foreign practitioners experienced in externally supported democratic transitions, and Danielle Reiff has framed the U.S. as now the recipient of that expertise rather than its exporter.
  • The United Democracy Alliance (UDA), described as a "big tent" coordinating the "blocking community," has developed red-line indicators and is standing up a "situation room."
  • Participants include a DSA/Marxist Unity Group Caucus member who called for an "explicitly anti-constitutional" organization — a view not endorsed by the other participants in that session, who were operating within a nonviolent, c3 framework.
  • Multiple speakers explicitly commit to nonviolence and constitutional process; one participant explicitly states: "This is not a concept for a secession."
What These Documents Do Not Show
  • No evidence of CIA direction, control, or funding of any element of this network.
  • No evidence of a plan to physically seize government, prevent Congress from convening, or remove officeholders by force.
  • No evidence that the network's "extraconstitutional process" language amounts to secession — Reiff's own formulation is a "Third Continental Congress" to draft a new declaration of principles if red lines are crossed.
  • No evidence that Indivisible, 50501, or No Kings organizations are being commanded rather than informed by this network's planning.

Section 4

The Three Emergency Scenarios — What the Network Is Planning For

Reiff laid out the three scenarios explicitly in the January 15, 2026 RAD Forum. Subsequent sessions developed each in greater detail.

Scenario 1 — Militarization
Defined as increased use of military, ICE, and National Guard on streets beyond current levels, potentially including Insurrection Act deployment to suppress voter turnout.
Documented Planning Activity
  • NSL4A led the detailed scenario work. RADM Smith's tabletop exercise (Washington, D.C.) modeled four Insurrection Act scenarios specifically calibrated to the 2026 midterms.
  • Most likely scenario assessed: ICE used to provoke civil unrest in blue cities, followed by Insurrection Act invocation, followed by military deployment to suppress voter turnout.
  • Counter-measures planned: Senate staff engagement to prepare public statements; election official checklists for "if the colonel shows up"; NSL4A war room to respond in real time.
  • Bryce Carpenter (former USAID, Democracy Renewal Group) confirmed co-leading RAD militarization scenario work alongside NSL4A contacts.

"So we we we looked at what if suddenly the president claims at a very high level of security of classification that that a foreign actor, be it Iran or China, has in fact corrupted those voting machines, and now he's sending the colonel to the voting site to confiscate the machine."

— RADM Michael E. Smith (Ret.), NSL4A, Oct. 14, 2026
Scenario 2 — Election Meltdown
Defined as a contested election outcome, including scenarios where results are disputed despite a clean election, or where newly-elected members of Congress are not seated promptly.
Documented Planning Activity
  • United Democracy Alliance developing "red lines that are now being refined in terms of indicators and metrics" specifically for election integrity scenarios.
  • International practitioner input solicited on election observation methodology: polling-station presence, legal hotlines, litigation strategy (Brazil, Serbia, Guatemala models discussed).
  • Melissa Hooper (RAD International Solidarity Working Group) describes value of aligned domestic and international voices in contesting disputed results.
  • John Mongan transmitted preparatory memos to governors in MA, CA, and IL regarding response options. CA and IL expressed conditional interest.

"We could have a perfectly clean election, and then half the country or 25 percent of the country doesn't trust the results."

— Danielle Reiff, RAD Forum, January 15, 2026
Scenario 3 — Constitutional Crisis / Extraconstitutional Response
Defined as an "acute constitutional crisis" — an attempt to undermine peaceful transition of power. If crossed, planners contemplate convening a "Third Continental Congress" to produce a new declaration of principles and, in Reiff's March 25 formulation, moving into an "extraconstitutional process."
Documented Planning Activity
  • R2T and RAD planning frames this as a contingency scenario triggered only if red lines are crossed — not as an unconditional agenda.
  • "Extraconstitutional process" is defined through the lens of a new public declaration of principles, not seizure of government or armed insurrection.
  • Mongan explicitly rejected the secession characterization: "This is not a concept for a secession."
  • The UDA "situation room" being developed by NSL4A is described as a monitoring and rapid-response capacity, not a command structure for operational action.
  • Nadine Bloch (Beautiful Trouble) acknowledged in the January session that the network must "sit with" the reality that clean transfer of power from right to left is unlikely without significant change — the most candid acknowledgment in the archive of the political limitations the network faces.

"We've talked about a sequence of… an important convening, maybe like a Third Continental Congress that actually comes up with a declaration that says here are the red lines. Like if these red lines in our democracy are crossed, we will move into an extraconstitutional process."

— Danielle Reiff, R2T Core Team Call, March 25, 2026
Assessment

The planning is reactive in structure — triggered by scenarios the network expects the administration to initiate — but proactive in preparation. Pre-organized red-line triggers, a standing war room, pre-positioned messaging for elected officials, and a pre-recruited roster of 1,600 retired national-security figures represent a level of operational readiness beyond normal civic advocacy. The framing throughout is defensive and nonviolent. The "extraconstitutional" language refers to public mobilization and constitutional convention-style deliberation, not to the use of force or the physical displacement of government.

Section 5

The International Dimension

The international dimension of this network is among its most analytically significant features. It is documented in Audio 3 (the international solidarity panel) and referenced in multiple other sessions.

What the International Dimension Consists Of
  • Reiff's self-positioning: She explicitly describes herself and the U.S. democracy advocacy community as having reversed roles — formerly exporters of pro-democracy methodology, now recipients of it. This is not metaphor; the panel includes practitioners from countries where election defense, polling-station presence, and international legitimacy signaling were operationally deployed against contested results.
  • Specific methodologies discussed: Polling-station presence by activists and legal observers (Serbia/CRTA model); hotlines providing legal assistance to voters in real time (Serbia); domestic-international observation mission coordination and a "situation room" tracking events (Brazil's 2022 Lula election model); litigation paired with street mobilization (Guatemala 2023 model).
  • International legitimacy function: Melissa Hooper articulates the value of coordinated domestic and international voices asserting that "we have seen this… bad act, and we think that it's impacted the election in this way" — explicitly connecting foreign attestation to domestic legitimacy claims.
  • Pre-bunking and inoculation: Rast'o Kuzel (MEMO 98, Slovakia) describes a methodology of anticipating false narratives before they dominate the information space and building "trusted channels between citizens and politics" — a media-counter-operations approach the network is incorporating.
Assessment

The international dimension is real and documented. The network is importing specific operational methodologies from countries where similar post-election crises occurred (Brazil 2022, Guatemala 2023, Slovakia, Serbia). This is not merely moral solidarity — it is tactical transfer. The Brazil model in particular — a coordinated situation room shared by domestic observation missions and international observers during a disputed election — maps directly onto the UDA "situation room" described in the August 13 session. No evidence of foreign funding, direction, or control of U.S. domestic planning has been documented in this archive. The influence runs in the methodological direction: U.S. groups adopting foreign practitioners' techniques.

Section 7

Bottom-Line Assessment

What Is Documented and Credible
  • A multi-organization network has been conducting structured contingency planning since at least January 2026 for three post-midterm emergency scenarios: militarization, election meltdown, and constitutional crisis.
  • The planning involves former senior U.S. diplomatic, USAID, and military officials in operational roles — not as commentators.
  • The network has achieved access to governors' forums and mayor networks and has transmitted planning memos to multiple governors.
  • NSL4A is standing up a war room and has conducted at least one in-person tabletop exercise specifically modeling Insurrection Act scenarios at the 2026 midterms.
  • The network is actively importing operational methodologies from countries where election defense was deployed (Brazil, Serbia, Guatemala, Slovakia).
  • The UDA "situation room," pre-organized red-line triggers, and pre-positioned messaging for elected officials represent a level of preparation exceeding routine civic advocacy.
What Is Not Supported by the Evidence
  • CIA direction, control, or funding — no evidence in any recording or document.
  • A plan to seize government, remove officials by force, or prevent Congress from convening.
  • A secession plan — explicitly rejected by a participant in the August 13 session.
  • An unconditional plan to declare the government illegitimate — all formulations in the recordings are conditional on red-line triggers.
Analytical Judgment
Bottom Line

The recordings document a coordinated, professional, and operationally serious effort to position a network of former U.S. officials and civil-society organizations to contest the post-2026 midterm environment across multiple lines of effort — legal, political, media, international, and public mobilization. The effort is larger, more organized, and more operationally advanced than routine political opposition. It is not, on the evidence of these documents, a coup, a CIA operation, a secession plan, or a plan to physically seize government. It is a defensive mobilization strategy built on the assumption that the administration will cross lines the network has pre-defined as constitutional violations — and that when it does, pre-positioned people, plans, messaging, and networks will be ready to respond. Whether that preparation is legitimate civic defense or pre-emptive destabilization is a political judgment the documents themselves do not resolve. What the documents resolve is that the preparation is real.

Section 8

European Nexus Assessment: Is There an Operational Link?

Analytical Question

Are the organizations documented in this archive operationally connected to the groups driving protests and civil unrest in Europe in 2026? This section assesses the evidence for and against a nexus, at three levels: shared intellectual framework, overlapping funding networks, and direct operational coordination.

A shared intellectual framework and overlapping historical funding networks create genuine ecosystem proximity between the U.S. Democracy Files network and European pro-democracy civil society. No operational command link between U.S. planners and European protest movements has been documented. European unrest in 2026 is primarily driven by domestic economic conditions and organized through decentralized social-media networks — not by NGO direction from any U.S. source.
Level 1 — Shared Intellectual Framework: Documented
  • Chenoweth doctrine is the connective tissue. Erica Chenoweth's nonviolent civil resistance framework — "Why Civil Resistance Works" and the 3.5% mobilization threshold — is the explicit academic foundation for the U.S. network's United Democracy Alliance concept. The same framework has been applied in European pro-democracy contexts, including training materials for Serbian and Slovak civil-society organizations in the period 2015–2023.
  • Beautiful Trouble's training methodology (Nadine Bloch, Execution tier) is used across the U.S. and European protest-training ecosystems. Its curriculum — direct action, nonviolent escalation, creative disruption — is publicly documented and widely distributed, not proprietary to the U.S. network.
  • The "color revolution" analytical template is applied by right-leaning commentators on both sides of the Atlantic to characterize both the U.S. Democracy Files network and European protest movements. The application is symmetrical and similarly contested in both contexts: the framework describes U.S.-backed regime change operations in the former Soviet sphere; its application to spontaneous domestic protest movements is analytically contested.
Level 2 — Overlapping Historical Funding Networks: Documented with Caveats
  • NED and USAID historical funding overlap is real. The National Endowment for Democracy (NED) and USAID have historically funded both MEMO 98 (Slovakia) and CRTA (Serbia) — the two organizations present in Audio 3's international solidarity panel. This creates a shared institutional history between the U.S. organizations (many of whose leaders are former USAID officials) and the European practitioners invited to advise them.
  • CRTA funding allegations: Serbian state media (Novosti, a partisan outlet) has alleged USAID specifically funded CRTA. This has not been independently confirmed. It is treated here as unverified but on the record. CRTA's confirmed funders include European foundations; USAID involvement is alleged but not documented in open sources available to this report.
  • This is historical funding, not current operational direction. Past USAID or NED grants to MEMO 98 or CRTA describe a funding relationship in their election-observation capacity, not current command authority over their participation in the U.S. Democracy Files planning sessions.
Level 3 — Operational Coordination with 2026 European Protests: Not Documented
  • European protests in 2026 are primarily domestically driven. France (pension reform, fuel costs), Spain (housing), Italy (wages and labor rights), Portugal (cost of living), Albania (anti-government, EU accession frustration), and Serbia (anti-corruption) — each is responding to discrete domestic political and economic conditions that substantially pre-date any 2026 U.S. election cycle activity.
  • Organization is decentralized and social-media native. The 2026 European protest wave is characterized by Gen Z organizing on TikTok, Instagram, and Discord — decentralized, leaderless, and platform-driven. This structure is inconsistent with top-down NGO direction from any external source.
  • No documented coordination signal. None of the five session transcripts reference European protest activity, coordination with European protest groups, or intent to support or direct European street action. The international solidarity panel (Audio 3) is explicitly a methodology-import exercise — U.S. practitioners receiving advice from Europeans, not the reverse.
  • The "international legitimacy" function Hooper describes is about foreign observers attesting to U.S. election integrity — not about U.S. organizations directing European action.
Nexus Assessment

The honest answer is: proximity, not command. The U.S. Democracy Files network and European pro-democracy civil society share a common intellectual tradition (Chenoweth nonviolent resistance doctrine), a common institutional history (NED/USAID funding of election-observation organizations in Central and Eastern Europe), and a common roster of individual practitioners who move between U.S. and European contexts. These create ecosystem overlap — enough that they appear, from the outside, as a unified network. They do not constitute operational coordination for European street action. The 2026 European protests are domestically generated responses to domestic economic conditions. The U.S. network documented here is focused on U.S. post-midterm scenarios, not on generating or directing European unrest. The "color revolution" frame — that both sets of events reflect a coordinated U.S. regime-change apparatus turned inward — is analytically coherent as a pattern-recognition exercise but is not supported by the operational evidence in this archive. The distinction between a shared framework and a command structure matters.
