The Democracy Files: A Primary-Source Assessment
What the recordings and documents actually show about post-election planning by a coordinated network of former U.S. officials, academics, and civil-society operators
Note on Sourcing

This report draws exclusively from primary source materials: audio recordings and their transcripts from five separate sessions between January and October 2026, and a persons-of-interest profile compiled from the same archive. Secondary source framing and amplification are not assessed in this report; this report covers what the primary sources say. This report does not adjudicate the framing of any secondary source; it reports what the primary sources say, in context.

Section 1

Bottom Line

What Is Documented and Credible
  • A multi-organization network has been conducting structured contingency planning since at least January 2026 for three post-midterm emergency scenarios: militarization, election meltdown, and constitutional crisis.
  • The planning involves former senior U.S. diplomatic, USAID, and military officials in operational roles — not as commentators.
  • The network has achieved access to governors' forums and mayor networks and has transmitted planning memos to multiple governors.
  • NSL4A is standing up a war room and has conducted at least one in-person tabletop exercise specifically modeling Insurrection Act scenarios at the 2026 midterms.
  • The network is actively importing operational methodologies from countries where election defense was deployed (Brazil, Serbia, Guatemala, Slovakia).
  • The UDA "situation room," pre-organized red-line triggers, and pre-positioned messaging for elected officials represent a level of preparation exceeding routine civic advocacy.
What Is Not Supported by the Evidence
  • CIA direction, control, or funding — no evidence in any recording or document.
  • A plan to seize government, remove officials by force, or prevent Congress from convening.
  • A secession plan — explicitly rejected by a participant in the August 13 session.
  • An unconditional plan to declare the government illegitimate — all formulations in the recordings are conditional on red-line triggers.
Analytical Judgment
Bottom Line

The recordings document a coordinated, professional, and operationally serious effort to position a network of former U.S. officials and civil-society organizations to contest the post-2026 midterm environment across multiple lines of effort — legal, political, media, international, and public mobilization. The effort is larger, more organized, and more operationally advanced than routine political opposition. It is not, on the evidence of these documents, a coup, a CIA operation, a secession plan, or a plan to physically seize government. It is a defensive mobilization strategy built on the assumption that the administration will cross lines the network has pre-defined as constitutional violations — and that when it does, pre-positioned people, plans, messaging, and networks will be ready to respond. Whether that preparation is legitimate civic defense or pre-emptive destabilization is a political judgment the documents themselves do not resolve. What the documents resolve is that the preparation is real.

Section 2

Indicators to Watch: Signals That Would Confirm Destabilization Intent

Analytical Note

The infrastructure documented in this archive — legal defense funds, trained marshals, bail networks, legislative relationships, international solidarity contacts — is consistent with robust, lawful protest organization. It is also consistent with pre-positioned capacity for something larger. The following indicators are the observable signals that would distinguish disciplined civic organizing from coordinated destabilization. None of these indicators is currently confirmed; they constitute a watch list for readers who wish to monitor how this situation develops.

Watch for rapid multi-city mobilization on short notice, hardening operational security, pre-deployment of legal infrastructure before announced events, synchronized messaging across nominally independent organizations, and logistics-level contact between domestic and European networks. Any combination of three or more indicators appearing in close temporal proximity would significantly elevate the assessment of coordinated intent.
1 — Escalation in Trigger Discipline

Organic protest movements require time to organize — days or weeks between a triggering event and street action. A shift to rapid multi-city mobilization within hours of a triggering event suggests pre-positioned activation rather than spontaneous response. Watch particularly for mobilization at a scale or geographic spread inconsistent with the apparent magnitude of the trigger.

2 — Pre-Deployment of Legal Infrastructure

The bail funds and legal observer networks documented in Section 6 are reactive by design — they respond to arrests. Watch for legal observers and rapid-response legal teams deployed to cities in advance of any announced event. Pre-deployment is a planning tell: legal infrastructure goes forward only when organizers expect arrests before the event is publicly announced.

3 — Hardening Operational Security

The Democracy Files network operates publicly — its training sessions, policy documents, and organizational relationships are in open sources. A shift to encrypted coordination platforms, participant vetting, and compartmentalized communications for activities that were previously conducted openly would indicate a change in operational intent, not just operational method.

4 — Synchronized Cross-Organization Messaging

Independent organizations naturally produce varied messaging about the same events — different emphasis, language, and framing. Identical or near-identical language, hashtags, and narrative frames appearing simultaneously across nominally independent organizations is a command-and-control indicator. Organic solidarity produces convergent themes; coordinated action produces verbatim convergence.

5 — New Funding Streams Tracing to European Networks

Section 7 documents historical NED/USAID funding overlap as the common institutional thread between U.S. and European civil society. Watch for new funding relationships — particularly in the 90-day window preceding an election or major legislative vote — that trace to European activist foundations or their U.S. intermediaries. Funding cycles preceding operational cycles is a documented pattern in coordinated civil action.

6 — Legislative Coordination with Street Action

The Horizons Block-Bridge-Build framework explicitly cultivates relationships with sympathetic legislators. The European precedent includes instances of unusual legislative scheduling — recesses, quorum breaks, procedural delays — timed to coincide with street action in a way that prevents legislative response. Watch for legislative scheduling anomalies that create a window of reduced institutional capacity during periods of planned street mobilization.

7 — Logistics-Level Contact with European Networks

The international solidarity panel (Audio 3) brought MEMO 98 and CRTA to the table in an advisory capacity — European practitioners sharing methodology with U.S. organizers. That is ideology transfer. Watch for a qualitative shift: operational or logistics-level personnel from European protest networks appearing at U.S. planning events, or U.S. network leaders traveling to European operational centers outside of conference or training contexts. The distinction between a conference visitor and a logistics coordinator matters.

8 — Nakagawa Threshold Crossing

Scot Nakagawa (22nd Century Initiative) introduced a probability framework in the June 30, 2025 post-conference huddle that functions as a built-in decision metric: administrative-state resistance alone = <9% probability of blocking authoritarian consolidation; civil resistance = approximately 50/50; organized labor involvement = >80%. The framework frames labor as the decisive variable — the next escalation step. Watch for coordinated outreach by network principals to major union leadership or active AFL-CIO/SEIU engagement with R2T/RAD planning sessions. Simultaneously, Nakagawa cited approximately 8 million No Kings Day attendees as roughly 2.4% of the U.S. population — short of the Chenoweth 3.5% threshold (~11.6 million). Watch for mobilization events that plausibly approach or breach the 3.5% threshold; the network is tracking this number as a live operational metric.

Assessment — Updated Oct. 22

One indicator has moved from "watch" to "partially confirmed." The August 5 session documents RADM Smith explicitly describing the situation as "everybody's already pre-organized" and acknowledging R2T provides "the day after tomorrow plan." Indicators 1 (trigger discipline) and 3 (OPSEC hardening — legal counsel advised against recording, subpoena risk assessed as real) also have partial confirmation. The Nakagawa framework (Indicator 8) introduces a quantifiable escalation threshold: if organized labor joins at scale, the network's own internal probability model shifts the assessment of success from coin-flip to likely. The remaining indicators — new European funding streams, legislative coordination anomalies, logistics-level European contacts, and Nakagawa threshold crossing — remain unconfirmed. The overall assessment: the infrastructure is pre-positioned and the trigger conditions are identified. Watch the midterm period, November 2026 through January 2027, for labor outreach (Indicator 8) and threshold-scale mobilization events (Indicator 8 / Chenoweth metric).

Section 3

Overview and Bottom-Line Assessment

A network of former U.S. diplomats and USAID officials — many from USAID's Democracy, Rights, and Governance (DRG) office, shut down in 2025 — retired military flag officers, academics, and civil-society operators has been conducting coordinated planning since at least January 2026 for scenarios in which a Democratic midterm sweep triggers a sustained campaign against the Trump administration. The planning includes pre-organized street mobilization, engagement with governors and mayors, international solidarity coordination, and contingency framing for events they describe as crossing constitutional "red lines." Its intellectual and operational lineage traces directly to the USAID democracy-assistance playbook used in externally supported democratic transitions abroad — including a confirmed connection to Canvas, the Serbian activist training organization that operationalized the Otpor model in more than 50 countries. The planning is real, organized, and more operationally advanced than routine political opposition. It does not, on the evidence of these recordings, constitute a coup, a secession plan, or a CIA-directed operation. Three findings from the newest primary sources materially advance the assessment: (1) the Chenoweth 3.5% mobilization threshold is being tracked as a live operational metric — current mobilization is estimated at approximately 2.4% of the U.S. population and approaching the threshold; (2) a Washington State Secretary of State participated in a September 2026 briefing that explicitly discussed provisional or transitional governance if the federal government is declared illegitimate, naming governors and former presidents as potential transitional actors; and (3) Ezra Levin of Indivisible confirmed on record that Chenoweth has been "the intellectual foundation for a lot of the strategy of the last 20 months." The problem is structural: people who spent careers deciding when foreign governments had lost legitimacy have written that test for the United States, in private, with no public debate, and are actively tracking whether the conditions for applying it have been met.
What These Documents Show
  • A named network — operating under the Renew to Thrive (R2T), Renew American Democracy (RAD), Democracy Renewal Group, and National Security Leaders for America (NSL4A) umbrellas — has developed a three-tier structure: doctrine/research at the top, former national-security operators in the middle, and protest/legal/street execution below.
  • The network has identified three "emergency scenarios" — militarization, election meltdown, and constitutional crisis — and conducted planning sessions, breakout groups, and at least one in-person tabletop exercise in Washington, D.C. for each.
  • Planners have engaged or sought to engage governors (through "Governors Safeguarding Democracy"), mayors, Senate staff, and election officials with preparatory materials and briefings.
  • The network has explicitly solicited input from foreign practitioners experienced in externally supported democratic transitions, and Danielle Reiff has framed the U.S. as now the recipient of that expertise rather than its exporter. Canvas — the Serbian activist training organization that grew from Otpor and trained activists in more than 50 countries — was included in the 2023 USAID coalition that received that office's largest single grant award. That award is the direct institutional link between the USAID DRG playbook and the current domestic operation.
  • The RAD framework has four named components: Block (delegated to the UDA), Bridge and Belong, Win, and Build — the last explicitly framing constitutional redesign as the end-state goal, with crisis as the opening: "We can take that breakdown moment and leverage it for a big structural reform — a refounding moment." (Speaker 2, DataRepublican Oct. 22 transcript.)
  • Legal counsel advised network members to stop recording planning sessions in case of subpoenas. Most members continued recording anyway, producing the archive now in public circulation. The fact that subpoena risk was assessed as real enough for legal counsel to raise it is material to any assessment of the network's legal posture.
  • The United Democracy Alliance (UDA), described as a "big tent" coordinating the "blocking community," has developed red-line indicators and is standing up a "situation room." The UDA is explicitly identified as Erica Chenoweth's project; its meetings are secret, it has no public website or filings, and it declined to appear at RAD forums. Reiff characterizes it as "progressive coded… like that on steroids."
  • The Chenoweth 3.5% mobilization threshold is being tracked in real time as an operational metric. Scot Nakagawa (22nd Century Initiative) reported on June 30, 2025 that approximately 8 million people mobilized on the last "No Kings Day" — placing current mobilization at approximately 2.4% of the U.S. population and approaching the documented threshold. Nakagawa also cites a Chenoweth-derived probability framework: civil resistance alone shifts the odds of blocking an authoritarian takeover from 9% (administrative state alone) to 50/50; with organized labor, to over 80%.
  • The network has briefed at least one sitting state official on its emergency planning scenarios: Washington State Secretary of State Steve Hobbs participated in the September 8, 2026 "Thriving Together Washington State Civic Innovation Forum" at which Reiff laid out all three emergency scenarios and explicitly discussed provisional or transitional governance — including "a cross-partisan group of governors" and former Presidents Bush and Obama playing a stabilizing role if the federal government is declared illegitimate. Hobbs responded warmly and connected the scenarios to conversations he had with German and Japanese parliament members about democratic backsliding in their countries.
  • Reiff's Washington State briefing includes the most explicit articulation of post-illegitimacy governance planning in the archive: "How can cross-partisan political leaders come together to think about provisional or transitional leadership of the country in case the federal government becomes illegitimate?" This goes beyond the "Third Continental Congress" framing in earlier sessions to name specific actors — governors and former presidents — in a transitional role.
  • Participants include a DSA/Marxist Unity Group Caucus member who called for an "explicitly anti-constitutional" organization — a view not endorsed by the other participants in that session, who were operating within a nonviolent, c3 framework.
  • Multiple speakers explicitly commit to nonviolence and constitutional process; one participant explicitly states: "This is not a concept for a secession."
What These Documents Do Not Show
  • No evidence of CIA direction, control, or funding of any element of this network.
  • No evidence of a plan to physically seize government, prevent Congress from convening, or remove officeholders by force.
  • No evidence that the network's "extraconstitutional process" language amounts to secession — Reiff's own formulation is a "Third Continental Congress" to draft a new declaration of principles if red lines are crossed.
  • No evidence that Indivisible, 50501, or No Kings organizations are being commanded rather than informed by this network's planning.

Structural Assessment

The network demonstrates functional specialization: doctrine and academic legitimacy at the top tier; former U.S. government national-security and diplomatic operators in the middle tier providing credibility, institutional access, and operational planning; and training, legal, and street-mobilization capacity at the execution tier. This is not an improvised coalition. It is an organized, hierarchical planning structure with distinct roles assigned at each tier.

The September 8, 2026 Washington State forum marks a material escalation in government-official engagement. Previous engagements were with elected officials in politically sympathetic forums (Democratic governors, mayors) or with legislative staff. Washington State Secretary of State Steve Hobbs is the first confirmed sitting state constitutional officer documented receiving the full three-scenario briefing — including the provisional governance framing. His positive response and reference to parallel conversations with foreign parliaments suggests this was not an isolated outreach.

The Three Emergency Scenarios — What the Network Is Planning For

Reiff laid out the three scenarios explicitly in the January 15, 2026 RAD Forum. Subsequent sessions developed each in greater detail.

Scenario 1 — Militarization
Defined as increased use of military, ICE, and National Guard on streets beyond current levels, potentially including Insurrection Act deployment to suppress voter turnout.
Documented Planning Activity
  • NSL4A led the detailed scenario work. RADM Smith's tabletop exercise (Washington, D.C.) modeled four Insurrection Act scenarios specifically calibrated to the 2026 midterms.
  • Most likely scenario assessed: ICE used to provoke civil unrest in blue cities, followed by Insurrection Act invocation, followed by military deployment to suppress voter turnout.
  • Counter-measures planned: Senate staff engagement to prepare public statements; election official checklists for "if the colonel shows up"; NSL4A war room to respond in real time.
  • Bryce Carpenter (former USAID, Democracy Renewal Group) confirmed co-leading RAD militarization scenario work alongside NSL4A contacts.

"So we we we looked at what if suddenly the president claims at a very high level of security of classification that that a foreign actor, be it Iran or China, has in fact corrupted those voting machines, and now he's sending the colonel to the voting site to confiscate the machine."

— RADM Michael E. Smith (Ret.), NSL4A, Oct. 14, 2026
Scenario 2 — Election Meltdown
Defined as a contested election outcome, including scenarios where results are disputed despite a clean election, or where newly-elected members of Congress are not seated promptly.
Documented Planning Activity
  • United Democracy Alliance developing "red lines that are now being refined in terms of indicators and metrics" specifically for election integrity scenarios.
  • International practitioner input solicited on election observation methodology: polling-station presence, legal hotlines, litigation strategy (Brazil, Serbia, Guatemala models discussed).
  • Melissa Hooper (RAD International Solidarity Working Group) describes value of aligned domestic and international voices in contesting disputed results.
  • John Mongan transmitted preparatory memos to governors in MA, CA, and IL regarding response options. CA and IL expressed conditional interest.

"We could have a perfectly clean election, and then half the country or 25 percent of the country doesn't trust the results."

— Danielle Reiff, RAD Forum, January 15, 2026
Scenario 3 — Constitutional Crisis / Extraconstitutional Response
Defined as an "acute constitutional crisis" — an attempt to undermine peaceful transition of power. If crossed, planners contemplate convening a "Third Continental Congress" to produce a new declaration of principles and, in Reiff's March 25 formulation, moving into an "extraconstitutional process."
Documented Planning Activity
  • R2T and RAD planning frames this as a contingency scenario triggered only if red lines are crossed — not as an unconditional agenda.
  • "Extraconstitutional process" is defined through the lens of a new public declaration of principles, not seizure of government or armed insurrection.
  • Mongan explicitly rejected the secession characterization: "This is not a concept for a secession."

"We've talked about a sequence of… an important convening, maybe like a Third Continental Congress that actually comes up with a declaration that says here are the red lines. Like if these red lines in our democracy are crossed, we will move into an extraconstitutional process."

— Danielle Reiff, R2T Core Team Call, March 25, 2026

Section 7

International Dimension and European Nexus

The network is importing specific operational methodologies from countries where election defense was deployed (Brazil, Serbia, Guatemala, Slovakia). The institutional lineage — USAID DRG → Canvas → Democracy Renewal Group — is confirmed, not analogical. No operational command link between U.S. planners and European protest movements has been documented. European unrest in 2026 is primarily domestically driven and decentralized.
What the International Dimension Consists Of
  • Tactical import, not solidarity: Reiff explicitly describes the U.S. network as having reversed roles — formerly exporters of pro-democracy methodology, now recipients. The panel includes practitioners from Serbia (CRTA), Slovakia (MEMO 98), Brazil, and Guatemala — countries where election defense was operationally deployed against contested results.   • Organization is decentralized and social-media native. Gen Z organizing on TikTok, Instagram, and Discord — leaderless and platform-driven — is structurally inconsistent with top-down NGO direction from any external source.

Nexus Assessment — Updated Oct. 22

The institutional lineage is confirmed: the playbook now operating domestically is the same playbook deployed in Serbia, Georgia, Ukraine, Egypt, and 50-plus other countries.
