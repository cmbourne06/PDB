Topic 1 — National Security & Foreign Affairs

National Security & Foreign Affairs · On-Demand · Update to 01 Nov report

Nigeria's anti-Christian terror campaign kills thousands annually with no effective government response

Change Since Last Report

Since 1 Nov, Fulani militant attacks have continued without pause across Plateau, Benue, and Kaduna states, killing at least 32 documented Christians in three weeks. President Tinubu made a 14 Nov political-rally appearance in Jos — the first visit to Plateau since April — apologized for not visiting attacked communities on a prior trip, and promised reconstruction; Fulani militants killed 14 more Christians in Plateau in the 72 hours that followed. The 3 Nov U.S. midterm elections returned a Democratic majority in both chambers of Congress; that result now governs the near-term trajectory of the aid-conditionality and CPC pressure campaign that is Watch item W1.1.

BLUF

Nigeria accounts for roughly 72% of Christians killed for their faith worldwide; attacks continued through the week of 17 Nov, with at least 14 killed in Plateau state alone. Tinubu's symbolic Jos visit produced no security improvement, and the Democratic Congress that takes office in January introduces new uncertainty about the sustained U.S. pressure on which the accountability framework depends.

Key Facts

  • Open Doors' 2026 World Watch List recorded 3,490 Christians killed in Nigeria during the reporting period (Oct 2024–Sep 2025) — 72% of the global total — giving Nigeria a maximum violence score of 100/100 and ranking it #7 worldwide (Open Doors International, primary document). Intersociety documented 3,083 killings and 3,193 abductions in Nigeria from January through August 2026 alone (Arise TV Nigeria, digital outlet).
  • Since 1 Nov, documented attacks include: 3 Nov — pastor and three others killed in a market attack in Benue; two Christians killed and 20 abducted in Kaduna; 8–9 Nov — nine killed and five abducted in Plateau; 15 Nov — nine Christians killed in Zwagal, Mangu, Plateau, and 12 Christians seized in Kaduna (three later escaped); 15–16 Nov — 14 Christians killed across four Plateau LGAs including Riyom, Barkin Ladi, Mangu, and Bokkos (International Christian Concern, digital outlet; Crossed Arrows International, digital outlet).
  • On 14 Nov, Tinubu attended an APC political rally at Zaria Road Stadium in Jos, Plateau state — his first visit since an April trip that drew criticism for being confined to the airport. He apologized for not visiting attacked communities during his earlier trip and pledged reconstruction aid and return of internally displaced persons to their ancestral homes. The Nigerian government's Ministry of Information published the apology as an official statement (Federal Ministry of Information Nigeria, official statement). Fourteen Christians were killed in Plateau within 72 hours of his departure (International Christian Concern, digital outlet).
  • On 3 Nov, Democrats swept the U.S. midterm elections, winning the House by a decisive majority and flipping at least seven Senate seats for a 54-seat majority. The new Congress convenes in January 2027. The incoming Democratic majority has not signaled support for the House-passed 100% aid withholding amendment or the CPC designation framework; the existing House bill still requires Senate passage and a presidential signature (This Day Live Nigeria, newspaper; The Guardian Nigeria, newspaper).
  • The Nigerian government has a reported $750,000-per-month contract with a U.S. lobbying firm to counter the "Christian genocide" narrative with conservative and evangelical audiences in Washington, and separately has retained the firm Valcour — led by a former State Department adviser — at $120,000 per month to influence Congress and the Trump administration (Nairametrics, digital outlet; Legit.ng, digital outlet).
  • A year after the CPC designation, Rep. Chris Smith told a congressional hearing: "The violence has not ended. Christian communities continue to face killings, abductions, attacks on churches and the destruction of their homes and livelihoods." Human rights groups cited at the hearing put the total killed or abducted in 2026 above 6,000 (Associated Press via KMPH, wire service).
  • Three overlapping groups drive the violence: armed Fulani militias (55% of Christian deaths), Boko Haram (6%), and ISWAP (2%). Fulani militias kill Christians at 6.2 per Muslim killed. Some analysts assess ISWAP is funding Fulani operations in the Middle Belt; this remains unconfirmed in open-source reporting (Spanish Ministry of Defence, primary document).

Points of Dispute

Is the violence religiously motivated or primarily driven by resource competition? The Tinubu government and security analysts cited by the BBC maintain that violence reflects multi-causal farmer-herder conflict over land and water, with religious identity incidental. Nigeria's Information Minister previously called Trump's warnings "unwarranted" and based on "faulty data" (The Guardian Nigeria, newspaper). Open Doors, the European Parliament, the U.S. Congress, and the Observatory for Religious Freedom in Africa cite the 6.2:1 Christian-to-Muslim kill ratio, the specific targeting of churches and clergy, and the timing of attacks during Christian holidays as evidence of deliberate religious targeting (Open Doors International, primary document). Open Doors' own senior researcher acknowledged some Muslims are also increasingly targeted by Fulani militants, a data point both sides cite (AllAfrica / BBC, digital outlet).

Does Tinubu's 14 Nov apology signal a genuine policy shift? The Tinubu government presented the visit as evidence of renewed federal commitment to Plateau — the Ministry of Information published the apology and promise of reconstruction as an official statement (Federal Ministry of Information Nigeria, official statement). Opposition politicians, including former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, argued the visit was timed to an APC political rally and that Plateau residents "may forgive but won't forget" his absence during periods of active killing (AllAfrica, digital outlet). The 14 additional deaths in Plateau in the 72 hours after Tinubu's departure are consistent with the view that the visit produced no security effect.

Will the Democratic Congress continue Republican pressure on Nigeria? Republican architects of the CPC and aid-conditionality framework — Cruz, Moore, Smith — built these mechanisms under a Republican-majority Congress. Democratic critics who testified at the November hearing raised concern that cutting aid undermined U.S. counterterrorism partnerships, with Rep. Sara Jacobs arguing: "Sabotaging our response does not demonstrate that the US is serious about helping Nigeria protect its citizens" (Associated Press via KATV, wire service). The House-passed 100% aid withholding bill has not been voted on by the Senate; whether the outgoing Republican Senate advances it before the new Congress takes over remains the near-term question.

Background

Nigeria's anti-Christian violence operates across three theaters: the northeast (Boko Haram and ISWAP since 2009); the Middle Belt (Fulani militia raids on Christian farming villages, escalating since roughly 2015); and the north (Sharia law in 12 states since 2000, codifying second-class status for Christians). Nigeria climbed from #12 on the Open Doors World Watch List in 2020 to #7 in 2026, with a maximum violence score. The U.S. designated Nigeria a Country of Particular Concern in Oct 2025; the European Parliament adopted an emergency resolution 510–1 in July 2026; a UN Special Rapporteur visited in June 2026. The U.S. House passed a 100% aid-withholding bill in July 2026; the Senate has not voted. The 3 Nov Democratic sweep of Congress is the most significant structural change to the accountability framework since the CPC designation (Congressional Research Service R47052, primary document).

Watch

  1. Will the outgoing Republican Senate pass the House-approved 100% aid-withholding bill before the new Democratic Congress is seated in January, and if not, will the Democratic majority maintain or dismantle the accountability framework? Assessment: Unlikely the Senate passes the House bill before the new Congress convenes (moderate confidence), based on the compressed legislative calendar and the death of Senator Graham — previously a key foreign-aid advocate — which removes a potential coalition builder; the incoming Democratic majority has signaled skepticism about punitive aid conditions and is unlikely to advance a new version of the House bill.
  2. Will Tinubu's pledges from the 14 Nov Jos visit — reconstruction funds, IDP returns, strengthened security — produce any measurable reduction in Fulani militia operations in Plateau state within 90 days? Assessment: Very unlikely within 90 days (moderate confidence), based on the pattern of prior government announcements — including the April 2026 pledge of 5,000 AI-enabled surveillance cameras in Plateau — that have not been followed by documented reduction in attack rates, and the continuation of lethal attacks within days of the November visit.
  3. Will the UN Special Rapporteur's formal report on her June 2026 Nigeria visit produce enforceable international pressure, or will it enter the body of unacted-upon documentation? Assessment: Unlikely to produce near-term enforcement (moderate confidence), based on the absence of a Security Council pathway and the pattern of prior UN resolutions and EP resolutions producing no behavioral change in Abuja; the report may sharpen the evidentiary record for future sanctions proceedings.
