Poland's Tribunal Standoff: Rival Court Leaderships, Coalition Strain, and a No-Confidence Bid

BLUF

Poland's government and president remain deadlocked over the Constitutional Tribunal, which now has two rival leaderships, and neither side has offered a concession. The fight has moved from the court building into parliament, where the governing coalition's cohesion and an opposition no-confidence bid now decide whether the government can hold its course.

Change Since Last Report

Since the 31 Oct European unrest update, the president's crisis talks ended without agreement and Brussels took a public position. The assessment shifts: Sejm arithmetic, not physical control of the court, is now the decisive variable.

Key Facts

  • On 30 Oct, Anna Korwin-Piotrowska ordered a one-month suspension of Chief Justice Bogdan Święczkowski and his deputy and claimed the acting chief's post. The Tribunal's official account says she is not a judge and Święczkowski remains in office (Euronews; Euronews, 30 Oct; Reuters).
  • Seven Sejm-elected judges entered the building under State Protection Service escort; police kept Święczkowski out and detained three protesters after the fence was breached. Several hundred protesters returned on 31 Oct (Notes from Poland; AP; CNN).
  • Coalition partners PSL and Poland 2050 joined opposition parties at President Nawrocki's 31 Oct crisis talks; Tusk's Civic Coalition stayed away. No agreement emerged (Al Jazeera; CNN).
  • Former Prime Minister Mateusz Morawiecki announced a constructive no-confidence motion naming former Sejm speaker Marek Jurek to lead a technical government until the 2027 election. Such a motion requires 231 votes and a named successor (bne IntelliNews; Constitution, Art. 158).
  • A European Commission spokesperson said electing the eight new judges lies within the Sejm's power, cited the ECtHR interim order requiring their admission, and urged Nawrocki to accept them (Kyiv Independent; Al Jazeera; Venice Commission).
  • U.S. Ambassador Tom Rose warned that the crowds made the situation more combustible and urged calm on all sides; Breitbart reports he said interfering is not America's role (CNN; Breitbart).

Points of Dispute

Legality of the 30 Oct takeover. Tusk and the government say the new judges were lawfully elected and that restoring the rule of law after PiS rule requires decisive action (Fox News; Euronews). Nawrocki, PiS and the Tribunal's leadership call it a forcible takeover of a constitutional body (Reuters). Supreme Court chief Zbigniew Kapiński called it a coup, and Marcin Matczak, a critic of PiS's judicial changes, said the cited legal basis does not exist (Notes from Poland). No primary source settles this; the court that would rule is itself the object of the dispute.

Whether the president may withhold the oath. Nawrocki cites the legal requirement that judges swear the oath before the president and a 2026 Tribunal ruling that he is not obliged to receive it (Notes from Poland). The government rejects that ruling and relies on the Venice Commission opinion and the ECtHR order; the Tribunal itself held in 2015 that receiving the oath is the president's statutory duty (Tribunal, K 34/15). The president's office notes the Venice opinion is advisory (Notes from Poland). The authorities conflict, and none binds both camps.

Coalition partners and outside actors. Szymon Hołownia said forcing a fix would deepen the court's decline, while the Left refused the president's invitation (Notes from Poland). Tusk says pro-Russian groups and bots are exploiting the conflict; Marine Le Pen called the events a forcible takeover and faulted EU silence, and Foreign Minister Sikorski told her not to interfere (Euronews). The reporting reviewed documents EU statements, not direction of either camp.

Background

The dispute began in 2015, when the outgoing and incoming parliamentary majorities each elected Tribunal judges; PiS-era appointees then controlled the court, and in 2021 the ECtHR found its composition failed the test of a tribunal established by law (Euronews). Tusk's coalition took office in 2023 and does not recognize the court's rulings; Nawrocki, a PiS ally, was elected president in 2025 (CNN). In March 2026 the Sejm elected six judges; Nawrocki received two oaths and refused four, who swore in before the Sejm in April (Notes from Poland). The ECtHR ordered their admission in May; Święczkowski disputed its authority (Notes from Poland). Maciej Berek, whom Tusk called his right-hand man, joined the excluded group in October, bringing it to five (Notes from Poland).

Watch

  1. The 03 Nov General Assembly Korwin-Piotrowska called on the 2027 budget; the assembly also nominates Tribunal president candidates for presidential appointment (Constitution, Art. 194). Assessment: A session attended only by Sejm-elected judges is likely (moderate confidence), based on Święczkowski's 31 Oct pledge to regain control.
  2. Morawiecki's no-confidence motion. Indicator: any PSL or Poland 2050 statement of support. Assessment: Passage is very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on seat arithmetic: Rozwój Plus holds about 40 seats and needs PiS, both Confederation parties and coalition defectors.
  3. The Sejm vote on sending central bank governor Adam Glapiński before the State Tribunal, reported for early November alongside the 04 Nov rate decision (bne IntelliNews); it will show whether PSL and Poland 2050 still vote with the government.
