European Unrest: Scope, Network, and US Implications

A coordinated international network of at least 101 organizations spanning 13 countries — with explicit U.S. links — is amplifying and escalating civil unrest across Europe, with organized plans to replicate the campaign in the United States.

What began as localized student protests in France has been absorbed into a structured, externally amplified campaign by communist, socialist, Islamist, and far-left organizations operating across 12 European countries and the United States; a Shanghai-based American businessman under federal grand jury investigation for financial crimes provides the primary funding link between these organizations and the Chinese Communist Party.

Scope: Countries and Scale

  • Active unrest is documented in France, Spain, Italy, Portugal, Belgium, Albania, and Greece — seven countries with demonstrations ongoing as of 20 Oct 2026. Germany, Ireland, and the UK have organizations actively mobilizing for the Nov. 20 International Youth Strike.
  • An international coalition of 28 communist and socialist youth organizations across 12 European countries is organizing a coordinated strike on Nov. 20 under the slogan "Fight for Our Future, Not Their Wars," calling for walkouts, blockades, occupations, and mass demonstrations.
  • Fox News Digital's investigation identified 101 organizations across 13 countries — including the United States — coordinating, organizing, or amplifying the protest ecosystem as of Oct. 20. That figure is up from 54 organizations identified one week prior.
  • France is the epicenter: an estimated 500,000 demonstrators took to the streets Oct. 17; more than 6,500 have been arrested since protests began in mid-September. Organizers have scheduled "Acte II" for Oct. 24 (212 mobilizations planned), "Acte III" for Oct. 31, and further actions on Nov. 5 and Nov. 14.
  • Grievances driving the mass population are primarily domestic: cost of living, housing, fuel prices, education funding, and teacher shortages. These genuine grievances have been systematically exploited and amplified by organized external networks.

CCP and Singham Network Involvement

  • Neville Roy Singham, 72, sold his tech company ThoughtWorks for an estimated $785 million in 2017. He now lives in Shanghai, attends CCP workshops focused on overseas messaging, and has publicly expressed support for a "new world order" under Xi Jinping. He is married to CODEPINK co-founder Jodie Evans.
  • Singham funds and directs an array of U.S. far-left organizations including CODEPINK, The People's Forum, BreakThrough News, Tricontinental, the ANSWER Coalition, and the International Peoples' Assembly. These organizations are the primary U.S.-based nodes amplifying European unrest and planning domestic mobilizations.
  • BreakThrough News — which operates out of the same building as The People's Forum and shares staff — published advance coverage framing the European unrest as "Europe's Youth Uprising" and announced an online discussion on Oct. 20 titled "A rebellion is sweeping Europe." Journalists Rania Khalek, Zoe Alexandra, and Amanda Yee are among staff named in congressional correspondence as potential deposition targets.
  • Vijay Prashad, executive director of Tricontinental and a Singham-funded figure, publicly celebrated the European unrest.
  • CODEPINK co-founder Medea Benjamin launched a 12-day China tour on Oct. 13, promoting "revolutionary history" and "socialist development," while the federal grand jury investigation into Singham's network continues. Jodie Evans travels to Shanghai to visit Singham and participates in U.S. demonstrations alongside Benjamin.
  • The House Ways and Means Committee (Chairman Jason Smith, R-MO) issued subpoenas to The People's Forum, BreakThrough News, and Tricontinental in January 2026. All three organizations failed to produce a single document in response to earlier requests.

Follow the Money

  • A Fox News Digital five-part investigation documented approximately $278 million flowing from Singham through a Goldman Sachs donor-advised fund and two now-defunct shell companies to six core U.S. nonprofits.
  • Documented flows to core organizations: The People's Forum, $22.44 million; Tricontinental, $16.76 million; BreakThrough News (Breakthrough BT Media), $1.098 million; CODEPINK, approximately $1.3 million. Additional recipients include the Justice and Education Fund and the People's Support Foundation, which in turn funded the Party for Socialism and Liberation and the ANSWER Coalition.
  • Approximately $1.8 million from Singham-linked funding streams has been documented going to Chinese media outlets that echo CCP messaging.
  • The SDNY grand jury, authorized by Acting AG Todd Blanche and led by U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton, is investigating wire fraud, bank fraud, and money laundering. Grand jury subpoenas seeking bank records and other financial documents have been issued. No criminal charges have been filed.
  • The House Ways and Means Committee's investigation focuses on whether existing tax law adequately prevents foreign interests from using U.S. nonprofits to conceal funding sources and exercise influence. FARA (Foreign Agents Registration Act) compliance is a central concern. Singham and his organizations deny taking direction from the Chinese government.
  • Singham previously gave $1.3 million to CODEPINK; since 2017, approximately a quarter of CODEPINK's donations — totaling more than $1.4 million — have come from Singham-linked groups.
  • The congressional subpoena compliance deadline for the three key organizations was today, Oct. 20. Non-compliance triggers potential depositions of 21 officials and staff.

Points of Dispute

Key contested questions: Singham and CodePink deny that the organizations take direction from the Chinese government. Singham states he is guided by his own political beliefs; Benjamin states CodePink receives no money from the Chinese Communist Party directly. The World Socialist Web Site and left-wing commentators characterize the congressional investigation as a politically motivated witch hunt. Left-center and mainstream outlets (Reuters, Al Jazeera, Axios) report the underlying protests as genuine, largely domestic responses to cost-of-living grievances, noting the protests are not one coordinated European movement. The question of whether the Singham network constitutes foreign influence operations versus domestic political organizing protected by the First Amendment is the central legal dispute before the grand jury.
