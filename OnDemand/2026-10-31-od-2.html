Indications & Warnings

Confirmed · France · 31 Oct

Acte III concludes: 296 arrested, Molotov thrown in Paris; movement holds despite turnout decline from Acte II

Acte III drew 320,000 participants by Interior Ministry count and approximately 500,000 by organizer count — down from Acte II's 410,000 official / 800,000 organizer figures but sustained across France. 296 people were arrested nationwide (61 in Paris) and 5,114 security checks conducted in Paris alone. Violence was concentrated at the tail of the Paris march: hooded individuals threw paving stones and a Molotov cocktail at police; police responded with tear gas and baton charges. Tensions also reported in Nice, Toulouse, Marseille, and Rennes. Interior Minister Núñez described the situation as "generally calm" until noon, with violence emerging after the march officially dissolved. Student leaders said the government's offers — adjustments, not the repeal of Parcoursup they demanded — are inadequate. The movement has held three consecutive weekends; no sign of voluntary demobilization.

Breaking · Poland · 31 Oct

President Nawrocki physically confronts judges at Constitutional Tribunal in live-broadcast incident; Tusk vows "no compromise"

On the night of 30–31 Oct., President Karol Nawrocki cut short a state visit to Senegal and returned to Warsaw amid what his office called a "coup d'état" attempt at the Constitutional Tribunal. Video, broadcast live, showed Nawrocki pushing past judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska in the Tribunal doorway; Nawrocki's aide claimed the judges breached his presidential inviolability; Korwin-Piotrowska and judge Maciej Berek said Nawrocki barged in forcefully. Inside, Nawrocki told assembled judges: "You are not Constitutional Tribunal judges." Earlier that evening, parliament-chosen judges had entered the building; Korwin-Piotrowska announced she had suspended acting chief justice Bogdan Święczkowski and assumed leadership. Święczkowski's own supporters had cut through a fence to assist him; riot police held back further demonstrators. In a Saturday televised address, Tusk called the confrontation "scandalous" and said: "In certain matters there can be no compromise." He accused pro-Russian groups and Russian bots of exploiting the conflict online. Emergency consultations with parliamentary groups were called for Saturday afternoon.

Confirmed · Nov. 5

National union strike day in France — five major federations — now five days out; no Yellow Vest/student formal endorsement yet

CGT, CFDT, FO, Unsa, and FSU confirmed the Nov. 5 strike after the Oct. 21 Lecornu talks failed. Acte III's result — sustained turnout, no sign of demobilization, inadequate government response by student leaders' assessment — increases the probability of Yellow Vest and student federation co-endorsement of the strike before Wednesday. That endorsement would constitute formal multi-stream convergence for the first time in this protest cycle.

Acte III drew significantly below the organizers' own pre-event expectations, and below Acte II by both official and organizer measures. However, the movement has now sustained three consecutive Saturdays of six-figure turnout — a level of persistence the 2018–2019 Yellow Vest cycle took longer to establish. The Interior Ministry's own framing — "generally calm until noon" — acknowledges the morning was unremarkable; the afternoon violence was concentrated in specific areas after the march officially dissolved, with hooded individuals throwing paving stones and one Molotov cocktail at police. Most participants and student organizers have publicly condemned the violence.

Germany participated for the first time at scale: organizers reported 170,000 registered for solidarity protests in more than 30 German cities; police used water cannon in Cologne. In Spain, Barcelona police were on highest alert following the 130 arrests during Acte II-week action. Italy remained on alert after clashes in Milan, Turin, and Bologna. The transnational dimension of this cycle is now confirmed at the street level, not only in online solidarity gestures.

Student leaders stated after Acte III that the government had offered "mainly adjustments" and had not met the movement's demands, specifically the repeal of Parcoursup. PM Lecornu held several meetings with student unions during the week but announced no concrete institutional concession. The government's posture — engagement without capitulation — is producing diminishing returns: it has not demobilized the movement, and each passing week without a settlement advances the calendar toward the Nov. 5 union strike and Nov. 20 international strike.

Series Trajectory

Across three mobilization weekends, the official count has been: Acte I (17 Oct.) — figures not confirmed in this update; Acte II (24 Oct.) — 410,000 official / 800,000 organizer; Acte III (31 Oct.) — 320,000 official / ~500,000 organizer. The decline is notable but not yet determinative. Historical precedent from the 2018–2019 cycle shows Yellow Vest mobilization peaked at Acte IV before declining, with the movement persisting for months at lower turnout. The relevant question is whether Nov. 5 union strike participation changes the calculus by adding organized labor's mass weight to the street movement.

Poland — Constitutional Tribunal: From Standoff to Physical Confrontation

What this series described in Update 5 as a "genuine and serious institutional conflict between elected branches" has escalated into something more visible and volatile. On the night of 30–31 Oct., the following sequence occurred, corroborated by multiple sources including video:

The government's move: Parliament-chosen judges — those whose oaths President Nawrocki had refused to receive — entered the Constitutional Tribunal building. Judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska announced she had suspended acting chief justice Bogdan Święczkowski and assumed control of the Tribunal. This was the government's attempt to physically install its preferred court leadership.

The president's response: Nawrocki cut short a state visit to Senegal and returned to Warsaw. He arrived at the Tribunal, where video showed him pushing past Korwin-Piotrowska in the doorway. His aide, Marcin Przydacz, claimed the judges had breached the head of state's inviolability; Korwin-Piotrowska and judge Berek said Nawrocki barged in forcefully. Inside, Nawrocki addressed the judges directly: "You are not Constitutional Tribunal judges." Święczkowski's supporters separately cut through a fence to assist his re-entry; riot police held back additional demonstrators outside.

Tusk's framing: In a Saturday televised address, PM Tusk called the events "scandalous" and vowed "no compromise" on rule of law. He specifically warned that pro-Russian groups and Russian bots were exploiting the conflict online — invoking geopolitical framing that elevates the stakes of the domestic dispute and aligns it with the broader EU-Russia information environment.

Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek moved to lift Święczkowski's parliamentary immunity and called Nawrocki's conduct "shameful." Emergency inter-party consultations were called for Saturday afternoon; Poland 2050 (Hołownia's party) confirmed attendance; Kosiniak-Kamysz (deputy PM) said he would attend personally; the Left had not confirmed as of publication.

The constitutional and legal questions — whether the parliament-sworn judges are legitimate, whether Korwin-Piotrowska's suspension of Święczkowski is valid, whether Nawrocki had any legal standing to enter the building — are genuinely disputed and have no near-term judicial resolution, since the court itself is the object of the dispute. The Venice Commission has consistently sided with Tusk's general direction; Nawrocki and PiS reject its authority in this matter. The physical confrontation does not resolve these questions; it escalates the stakes for both sides.

Points of Dispute

Poland — "coup" framing: Nawrocki and the opposition characterize Tusk's judicial moves as a "coup d'état" or "forceful takeover." Tusk characterizes them as restoration of constitutional order after PiS's decade-long judicial capture. The right-wing framing ("coup") and the center-left framing ("rule of law restoration") reflect genuine disagreement about which constitutional order is being violated. This report does not adjudicate between them; both framings are presented because both are held by elected officials and have distinct legal bases.

French turnout gap: The 190,000 gap between official (320K) and organizer (~500K) turnout estimates for Acte III is the largest percentage disparity of the three weekends. Both counts show decline from Acte II. The Interior Ministry's methodology (police visual estimates, aerial counts) systematically undercounts dispersed crowds; organizer estimates systematically overcount. The true figure is between the two. The directional signal — decline, but sustained — is not disputed.

Poland — Tusk's Russia framing: Tusk's invocation of Russian bot exploitation in the context of a domestic judicial dispute is politically significant. It may be accurate — EU disinformation monitoring has documented Russian amplification of the Polish constitutional conflict since 2025 — but it also serves to delegitimize domestic opposition by associating it with foreign adversaries. Both things can be simultaneously true.
