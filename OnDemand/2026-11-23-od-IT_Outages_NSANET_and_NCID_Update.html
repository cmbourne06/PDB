November IT Outages, NSANet and the NCIC Intrusion: Status Update

BLUF

Officials say the November outages were neither coordinated nor state-sponsored, and the FBI has named no actor for the intrusion into its NCIC database. The Agriculture Department disclosed a separate breach with no known actor. No reporting ties any of these incidents to a Chinese Typhoon hacking group.

Change Since Last Report

New since 21 Nov: Clayton’s 22 Nov ABC interview, the USDA breach and continued FBI silence on NCIC. The assessment has not shifted; nothing new supports a Typhoon link.

Key Facts

  • DNI statement. On 22 Nov Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told ABC the week’s incidents were not coordinated or state-sponsored and pointed to dated digital systems. He declined to discuss reported attacks on the FBI and the Agriculture Department (ABC News; Daily Caller).
  • Outage record. Outages on 12–17 Nov hit Microsoft 365, FAA air traffic control, Chase, United and NSANet. Microsoft cited a capacity upgrade and Chase a hardware bug. The FAA said it had no indication of a cyberattack (AP; Newsweek; Al Jazeera).
  • Congressional hearing. On 18 Nov Clayton called the incidents “separate service outages” tied to maintenance and technical changes, and said Iranian threats have substantially increased, without linking them to the outages. Lawmakers in both parties voiced skepticism (Reuters; ABC News).
  • NCIC. CNN, AP and Fox News reported on 20 Nov an FBI investigation of an apparent intrusion into the National Crime Information Center, its criminal-records database. The FBI declined to comment. No actor or stolen data is public (CNN; AP; Fox News; IBTimes).
  • Earlier FBI breaches. The FBI called a March surveillance-system breach a “major incident” reached through a third party. ShinyHunters, a criminal group, claimed September’s jobs-portal compromise. No source ties either breach to NCIC (The Hill; Al Jazeera).
  • USDA. The Agriculture Department disclosed a security incident on 16 Nov, took numerous applications offline, including farmer payment tools, and reset all database passwords. Who carried it out and whether data was taken are unknown (Nextgov; ABC News).
  • Typhoon status. No outlet or agency has tied a November incident to a Typhoon group. Lumen reported on 28 Oct that Volt Typhoon built a new relay network, with no sabotage reported. China’s foreign ministry rejects American hacking attributions (Nextgov; Foreign Ministry).

Points of Dispute

Whether patching explains the wave. Clayton and the administration cite patches and upgrades; ABC notes the administration ties many outages to patches that artificial intelligence identified, a point Clayton did not address (ABC News). Lawmakers in both parties doubt the coincidence framing (ABC News). Techlicious notes the NSA’s statement named neither the flaw nor patching (Techlicious).

Whether NCIC connects to earlier FBI breaches. Fox News calls NCIC the second FBI cyberattack in months (Fox News). The Hill reports outlets suspected China-linked hackers in March, while the FBI cited a third party (The Hill). CNN lists September and a 2023 incident as precedents, not March (CNN). IBTimes finds no public connection, and Trump offered no evidence tying NCIC to the Russia investigation (IBTimes).

Whether a state actor is excluded. Clayton sees no indicia of state sponsorship for the outages and would not discuss the FBI and USDA incidents (Daily Caller). Dream found a China-nexus operator exploiting the PHP2Shell flaws, and Aviatrix says the activity mirrors Typhoon patterns; Dream names no group and no NSA link, and Unit 42 assesses a Chinese-speaking operator in the UTC+8 time zone (Dream; Aviatrix).

Whether the FBI and USDA incidents are related. ABC’s sources say both agencies were hit late last week (ABC News). Nextgov dates the USDA disclosure to 16 Nov, before NCIC surfaced, and does not mention NCIC (Nextgov). Clayton declined specifics on both, and no source links them.

Background

The National Crime Information Center holds fugitive and gun-check records that federal, state and local law enforcement share (Fox News). NSANet is the NSA’s classified internal network. PHP2Shell is a set of web-server flaws; the NSA urged users to patch or go offline (TweakTown). Clayton tied the NSANet failure on 17 Nov to patching for those flaws, while the NSA cited scheduled maintenance. Dream found a China-nexus operator exploiting them with AI tools. “Typhoon” is the label American security firms give Chinese state-linked hacking groups. Volt Typhoon hides access in critical-infrastructure networks for use in a crisis. Salt Typhoon breached telecommunications carriers and law-enforcement systems. Flax Typhoon runs botnets of compromised devices. Silk Typhoon exploits IT suppliers. Such access shows no effect until used, so a linkage needs a matching sector, access path, attribution and timing. No source supplies those for the November incidents.

Watch

  1. The FBI or DOJ names an actor, or a claimant emerges, for the NCIC intrusion by 31 Dec. Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on ShinyHunters claiming September’s breach within hours and the March breach staying officially unattributed.
  2. An American or allied agency names a PRC-linked actor for any November outage or the NCIC intrusion by 31 Dec. Assessment: Very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on officials citing patching and maintenance and no source attributing any incident to China.
  3. The FBI, USDA or CISA names a cause or actor for the USDA breach by 31 Dec. Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on USDA’s silence on an actor and Clayton’s refusal on 22 Nov to discuss specifics.
