Indications & Warnings

Major Significance

Poland's Tribunal fight has reached police and prosecutors; police loyalty and coalition unity decide what follows

On 30–31 Oct a group of Sejm-elected judges, reportedly escorted by State Protection Service officers behind a police cordon, announced the suspension of Tribunal president Bogdan Święczkowski. Justice Minister and Prosecutor General Waldemar Żurek filed motions to lift his immunity. President Karol Nawrocki turned back from a state visit, entered the building at about 01:00 on 31 Oct, and called the move a forcible takeover. Both camps now claim the Tribunal. The tests over the next two weeks: whether police commanders state their position, whether coalition partners PSL and Poland 2050 hold, and who chairs the Tribunal's scheduled 3 Nov assembly.

Topic 1 — National Security & Foreign Affairs

National Security & Foreign Affairs · On-Demand · Assessment

Poland's Constitutional Tribunal standoff: who holds which instruments, and what follows

BLUF

Each camp is using the instruments it controls. The government holds the Sejm, the prosecutor general and the police. The president holds the oath and the veto. The Tribunal's chief held the docket and the door. On 30–31 Oct the government moved from appointments to criminal process and a police cordon, and the president answered by entering the building and calling for talks. Outside courts and the European Commission back the Sejm's right to elect the judges. None of the outside statements collected endorses the method used on 30 Oct, and a constitutional scholar who opposes PiS says the legal basis for the suspension does not exist. Expect two rival claims to the Tribunal's leadership through 15 Nov. Force beyond the present cordon is unlikely; police loyalty is the variable that would change that. Confidence: moderate on the legal reading, low on the political outcome. Valid through 15 Nov 2026; reassess on 3 Nov.

2The government's aim has stronger legal support than its method.

Assessment: Likely (moderate confidence) that every outside body that rules upholds the Sejm's right to elect the eight judges. Likely (low confidence) that the 30 Oct suspension would not survive review under the provision cited.

Basis Art. 194 gives the Sejm the election, and the ECtHR, the EU's highest court and the Commission support it. On method, the Tribunal's notice cites Art. 130 of the common-courts law. Matczak, who criticizes PiS and calls Święczkowski the worst person to lead the Tribunal, says that provision does not reach it. The suspending judge is one of the five the president has not sworn in, and her seniority claim rests on her judicial career, not her Tribunal service.

Against The oath requirement is statutory, and the president's camp argues the Sejm cannot seat judges alone. The Venice Commission opinion on the president's powers was not read. No court has ruled on the suspension.

Changes if a court rules on the suspension, or the Venice Commission text supports the president's reading.

4Police loyalty is the decisive variable. As of 31 Oct it shows orders executed and rank-and-file doubts, not defiance.

Assessment: Unlikely (moderate confidence) that police refuse orders at scale by 15 Nov.

Basis The cordon held overnight. Police Chief Gen. Marek Boroń publicly backed officers on 19 Oct after a 16 Oct clash at the Tribunal. A police union's open letter of 31 Oct (unauthenticated) asks him to state publicly where the police stand. It says many Warsaw officers are young trainees, that officers elsewhere doubt the legality of acting at the Tribunal and fear exposure under Penal Code Arts. 127, 128 and 231, that families worry officers will be left alone facing accusations for carrying out commanders' orders, that morale has fallen, and that appeals to "procedures and regulations" could end as in 2018 and 2021. It cites an unseen letter by Tomasz Michułka, a senior officer, as showing the operation was planned earlier with police leadership. It reports no refusals.

Against The letter is an early indicator. Fear of prosecution is the mechanism that moves rank-and-file loyalty, and the Tribunal says all participants in the 30 Oct operation are committing the crime of coup d'état. The State Protection Service was reported on both sides of the door: escorting the judges and accompanying the president.

Changes if senior commanders speak publicly for or against, officers are named in criminal complaints, refusals are reported, or the State Protection Service splits between the president and the judges.

Basis Three questions separate here. Alignment: the government and the EU bodies share the goal of seating the elected judges (documented). Leverage: binding rulings and withheld funds have pressed on the Tribunal's composition since 2021 (documented). Direction: no public evidence shows EU bodies directed the 30–31 Oct operation, and the Commission stopped short of endorsing it. Open sources cannot show private coordination, so this finding covers the public record only.

Against Whether these rulings bind Polish institutions is the underlying dispute: the Tribunal ruled in 2021 that parts of the EU treaties conflict with the Constitution, while the EU courts and the government hold that EU law prevails. Each camp holds an electoral mandate: the 2023 Sejm majority and Nawrocki's 2025 election at 50.9%.

Changes if the Commission or Council of Europe endorses the method, funds are tied to the Tribunal's composition, or Washington takes a position.
