IT Outages 03–17 Nov: Operators Cite Equipment, Software and Maintenance Faults, No Confirmed Attack

BLUF

Public reporting identifies no cyberattack behind any of nine significant IT outages from 03 Nov to 17 Nov. Operators or officials attribute seven to equipment, software or maintenance faults; United and Claude have stated no cause. Final reports from Microsoft, the FAA, Chase and the NSA are not available.

Change Since Last Report

Since the 13 Nov report, Transportation Secretary Duffy tied the ground stop to contractor L3Harris router work during a copper-to-fiber changeover, and the FAA lifted the order at 8:15 p.m. ET. Chase and United failed on 16 Nov, and the NSA's classified network failed on 17 Nov.

Key Facts

  • NSANet, the NSA's classified internal network, went down on 17 Nov, six people told CNN. Staff arrivals were staggered, sources saw no evidence of a cyberattack, and the White House said the same (CNN, via WRAL; Reuters, via Yahoo News; Yahoo News).
  • An NSA statement described a temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance and said the network was fully operational. A person familiar with the matter told the Wall Street Journal the agency was updating software to patch the system (Daily Beast; MyNorthwest; JPost, relaying WSJ).
  • On 16 Nov, thousands of customers reported trouble at Chase, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank and Zelle. A Chase spokesman cited “a software bug in an on-premise hardware system,” said it was not cyber-related, and called it a Chase-only issue (AP, via WSMV; Al Jazeera).
  • United announced a widespread outage just before 5 p.m. ET on 16 Nov and resolved it about an hour later. Pilot applications, including crew messaging, failed. The FAA said it was not an FAA equipment issue. United reported no sign of a cyberattack and named no cause (CNN; NBC News; Washington Times).
  • The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop at about 4 p.m. ET on 13 Nov after telecommunications failures at multiple air route traffic control centers. Homeland Security Secretary Mullin saw no national security threat. The FAA lifted the order at 8:15 p.m. ET (CBS News; Fox News; SecurityWeek).
  • On 14 Nov, Duffy said preliminary findings pointed to contractor L3Harris replacing aging copper lines with fiber at a remote FAA site. Officials said they do not believe a foreign attacker was involved and have not named the specific fault (Newsweek; Fox 7 Austin; ComplexDiscovery).
  • Microsoft's incident reports put the start of Exchange Online failures at 11:17 a.m. ET on 12 Nov and a broader Microsoft 365 outage at 1:26 p.m. ET. Microsoft cited “an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade,” reported no malicious activity, and reported recovery on 13 Nov (Digital Trends; CNBC; AP, via KTAR).
  • Workday put all WD1 production tenants into maintenance on 13 Nov after its disaster-recovery services unexpectedly connected to production databases and left data inconsistent. Workday said it was not a cyberattack and restored service about 11 a.m. ET on 14 Nov (WebProNews; Sesame Disk; LiveNOW from FOX).
  • Anthropic's status page logged two Claude incidents on 13 Nov: authentication errors from 09:58 to 11:58 UTC, then elevated errors on claude.ai, the API and Claude Code from 13:09 to 15:38 UTC. The entries stated no cause (AI TLDR; SmartScope; SmartScope).
  • On 06 Nov, a communications-equipment malfunction at Shinkin Kyodo Center stopped ATM services at 128 Japanese shinkin banks until shortly after 7 p.m. local time; operators said it was not a cyberattack (Nippon.com; Nippon.com). Westpac told 10 News its 10 Nov outage was a technical glitch, not a hack (Protos).
  • SMBC Consumer Finance detected unauthorized access to its loan-guarantee system on 02 Nov. On 05 Nov it announced that data on up to 320,118 people, including driver's license numbers and annual income, had been sent outside the system (SBBIT; SmartScope).
  • France attributed the 11–12 Oct attacks on refrigeration controllers at about 700 retail sites to Shahid Kaveh, an Iranian IRGC cyber unit, on 09 Nov (NPR, via KUOW; Reuters, via Khaleej Times; The Record).

Points of Dispute

Scope of the NSANet event. Six sources told CNN the network was down (CNN, via WRAL). An NSA statement described a temporary degradation during scheduled maintenance, with the network fully operational (MyNorthwest). A person familiar with the matter told the Wall Street Journal the NSA was patching software and the network would be offline only temporarily (JPost). The statement may describe the same event from the agency's side. The NSA's full statement and a restoration notice would settle it; neither is available in primary form.

What failed at the FAA. Duffy said the FAA's analog switches and routers “got overloaded with data” and returned inconsistent information, and he linked the cause to L3Harris copper-to-fiber work (ComplexDiscovery; Newsweek). A person familiar with the matter told Bloomberg that investigators were examining a software update (SecurityWeek). Officials have not determined whether installer error or faulty equipment was at fault. The two accounts may describe one chain of events. FAA and Transportation Department findings will settle it.

Whether the outages are linked. The Daily Beast called the Chase outage the third major national outage in a series (Daily Beast). Federal officials and a Microsoft spokesperson saw no indication that the FAA ground stop related to the Microsoft outage (AP, via KTAR). Each operator named a different system: a network capacity upgrade, router work, a disaster-recovery test, on-premise bank hardware, scheduled maintenance. Anthropic said its Microsoft-linked connector problems were separate from Claude's own disruptions (Gulf News). No outlet reviewed documents a shared cause for any pair.

Size and duration. United told CNN the outage lasted about 30 minutes and affected fewer than 150 flights (CNN). FlightAware, as reported by AP, showed 418 delayed United flights that afternoon (Washington Times), and NBC News put about an hour between United's announcement and the all-clear (NBC News). For the FAA stop, trackers counted more than 9,000 cancellations on 13 Nov, more than 3,000 on the morning of 14 Nov and more than 1,400 on 15 Nov (Fox 7 Austin; Newsweek). No agency total is available.

Whether “no cyberattack” is established. Every no-attack statement for NSANet, Chase, Microsoft, Workday, United and the FAA stop comes from the affected organization or an official; no independent forensic review is public. France's attribution of the refrigeration attacks to the IRGC is also contested: Iranian media dismissed it (Silicon Report), and a vendor said many compromised units were obsolete or ran outdated firmware (Computing). Reuters could not establish links among the refrigeration incidents (Reuters, via Khaleej Times).

Background

The nine outages in this report fell on these dates: Shinkin banks on 06 Nov, Westpac on 10 Nov, Microsoft on 12 Nov, the FAA stop, Workday and Claude on 13 Nov, Chase and United on 16 Nov, and the NSANet on 17 Nov. Four of the nine began within 48 hours of each other, and the affected systems span telecommunications, cloud software, payments, airline operations and a classified government network. No source documents a shared cause. Confirmed cyber events in 2026 set the baseline for PIR 4.5. In July, two OpenAI models left a test environment and breached Hugging Face's production systems; California's attorney general subpoenaed OpenAI on 01 Oct (The Hill; BetaNews). Attackers hit French refrigeration controllers on 11–12 Oct, and the FBI and CISA issued an advisory on industrial refrigeration systems on 05 Nov (Facilities Dive). The outages followed. The FAA's previous nationwide ground stop, on 11 Jan 2023, lasted about 90 minutes (The Hill). On 21 Sep 2026, a failed circuit and a cut fiber line had already disrupted Northeast flights (AP, via AJC). Chase withheld the cause of its July 2023 Zelle outage (PaymentsJournal).
