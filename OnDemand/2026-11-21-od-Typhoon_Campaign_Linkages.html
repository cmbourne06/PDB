Chinese Typhoon Campaigns and the November Outages, NSANet and the NCIC Intrusion

BLUF

No source attributes the November IT outages, the NSA network failure or the intrusion into the FBI’s NCIC database to a Typhoon group, the label American firms give Chinese state-linked hackers. Operators and officials cite maintenance, software and equipment faults for the outages, and no technical detail on NCIC is public. Typhoon groups remain active against American targets in 2026.

Key Facts

  • Outages. On 12–17 Nov, outages hit Microsoft 365, FAA air traffic control, Chase, United and NSANet, the NSA’s classified network. Microsoft blamed a capacity upgrade, Chase a hardware bug and FAA officials contractor network work; none reported a cyberattack (AP; Newsweek; Al Jazeera).
  • NCIC. On 20 Nov CNN and AP reported an FBI investigation of an intrusion into the National Crime Information Center, the FBI’s national criminal-records database, by “apparent hackers.” No actor, entry point or stolen data is public (CNN; AP; Al Jazeera).
  • Earlier FBI breaches. The FBI called a March surveillance-system breach a “major incident.” ShinyHunters, a criminal group, claimed September’s jobs-portal compromise, and the FBI called its “point of breach” undetermined. No source read ties either to NCIC (The Hill; Al Jazeera).
  • NSANet. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton tied the 17 Nov failure to patching for PHP2Shell, a set of web-server flaws, and said officials “do not see the indicia of state-sponsored activity.” The NSA’s statement cited scheduled maintenance (Fox Business; CNN).
  • PHP2Shell. Dream found a China-nexus operator chained PHP2Shell with a kernel flaw to root 25 firewall servers, including a U.S. government-affiliated organization’s. Dream names no actor, no Typhoon group and no NSA link; Unit 42 tracks it as CL-UNK-1280 (Dream).
  • U.S. enforcement. On 26 Aug the Justice Department seized QTFY’s QScan and QTRouter platforms, which it says Chinese state-sponsored hackers used. Targets included NASA and the Federal Reserve. Advisory AA26-281A on Flax Typhoon describes data theft and no disruption (DOJ; FBI, CISA, NSA).
  • Beijing. On 27 Aug China’s foreign ministry rejected the QTFY allegations as unsupported and cited an alleged NSA attack on its National Time Service Center. No source read shows a Chinese statement on the November incidents (Foreign Ministry).

Points of Dispute

Whether the March breach was Salt Typhoon. NBC’s source says the intrusion resembled Salt Typhoon techniques; Fox News was told China is responsible (NBC; Fox News). Nextgov could not independently confirm a Chinese link (Nextgov). CNN’s NCIC report lists the September breach and a 2023 incident, not the March one (CNN). These reports cite no FBI attribution.

Whether PHP2Shell links NSANet to Chinese operators. Clayton says patching took systems down (Fox Business). The Record reports NSA officials called his account correct (The Record); Techlicious notes the NSA’s statement named neither the flaw nor patching (Techlicious). Dream mentions no NSA link (Dream). No source connects the two.

Whether “China-nexus” means a Typhoon group. Aviatrix says the PHP2Shell activity mirrors Salt and Volt Typhoon patterns (Aviatrix). Dream names no group, and Unit 42 assesses a Chinese-speaking operator in the UTC+8 time zone (Dream).

How to read Beijing’s denials. China’s foreign ministry calls U.S. attributions disinformation and cites alleged U.S. pre-positioning (Foreign Ministry; Global Times); Xinhua carried the same line on 14 Jul (Xinhua). Justice later corrected its release to say all listed agencies were targeted and only some were compromised (Al Jazeera).

Background

“Typhoon” is the label American security firms and agencies give Chinese state-linked hacking groups. Four matter here. Volt Typhoon places hidden access in critical-infrastructure networks to enable disruption in a crisis. Salt Typhoon breached telecommunications carriers and law-enforcement systems to collect data. Flax Typhoon runs botnets of compromised edge and consumer devices. Silk Typhoon exploits IT suppliers to reach their customers. Pre-positioning of this kind shows no visible effect until used, so a linkage claim needs a matching sector, access path, attribution and timing. A May advisory says Volt Typhoon seeks to disrupt functions and operations (Washington Times). Lumen reported on 28 Oct that Volt Typhoon built a new relay network, with no sabotage reported (Nextgov). Dream found the PHP2Shell operator used AI tools, including Claude Code, and no full autonomy.

Watch

  1. The FBI or DOJ names an actor, or a claimant emerges, for the NCIC intrusion by 31 Dec. Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on ShinyHunters claiming September’s breach within hours and the March breach staying officially unattributed.
  2. A U.S. or allied agency names a PRC-linked actor for any November outage or the NCIC intrusion by 31 Dec. Assessment: Very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on no source attributing either and officials citing patching and maintenance.
  3. CISA, NSA or a vendor ties the PHP2Shell cluster CL-UNK-1280 to a named Typhoon group by 31 Jan 2027. Assessment: Unlikely (low confidence), based on Dream and Unit 42 leaving the cluster unnamed.

Analyst Comment: No Typhoon Linkage Has Evidence; Quiet Access Would Leave None

Judgment. A Typhoon operation is unlikely to explain the November outages (moderate confidence): each has a stated cause, and quiet access shows no effect. An NSANet link is unlikely (low confidence). The NCIC actor cannot be ranked (low confidence).

Target fit does not separate state from criminal actors. NCIC holds law-enforcement data like that Salt Typhoon collected, and ShinyHunters took FBI data in September.

PHP2Shell is the one shared factor, and it runs through patching. A China-nexus cluster exploited it from late October while the NSA urged users to patch or go offline (TweakTown). Assessment: roughly even chance (low confidence) that this urgency shaped NSANet’s maintenance timing, based on Clayton’s patching account and that advisory. AI-assisted discovery of the flaws would raise patch volume (Implicator).

Absence of attribution is not clearance. Volt Typhoon rebuilt relay infrastructure after earlier takedowns (Nextgov), and court documents place a South Carolina power company among Flax Typhoon’s victims (The Register). The record supports no linkage and no exclusion.

Key assumptions. Operator and official causes are accurate, NCIC reporting is complete on attribution, and quiet pre-positioning leaves no visible effect before use. Disclosure: Anthropic made the model that prepared this report, and Claude Code appears in Dream’s findings.

Alternative outcome. FBI or CISA detail places PHP2Shell exploitation inside NSA or FBI systems, or ties NCIC to the March intrusion’s tooling. Either would move both judgments toward a state actor.
