Organizational Profiles

Background on all organizations referenced in the Democracy Files source series, organized by network layer as identified in the transcripts.

Doctrine Layer — Academic Infrastructure

Harvard Kennedy School / Ash Center — Civil Resistance Program

Intellectual Foundation · Doctrine Layer

The Ash Center for Democratic Governance and Innovation at Harvard Kennedy School houses the research program that produced the theoretical foundation for the Democracy Files network. Erica Chenoweth and Zoe Marks co-lead the center's civil resistance research strand. The program's output — including Chenoweth's Why Civil Resistance Works and her ongoing updates to the 3.5% threshold analysis — provides the empirical basis for the network's mobilization targets. Chenoweth's HKS faculty appointment (also Radcliffe Institute) gives her institutional standing that other network participants cite when framing their work as research-grounded rather than purely political. The Ash Center's public publications are open access, meaning the doctrine is simultaneously an academic product and an operational manual.

Strategy and Coordination Layer

Renew American Democracy (RAD)

Central Coordination Hub · Strategy Layer

RAD is the primary coordination mechanism documented across the Democracy Files transcripts. Co-led by Danielle Reiff and Paul Zeitz, it operates as the strategic planning and inter-network coordination body for the broader ecosystem. RAD defined and developed the three emergency scenario framework (militarization, election meltdown, acute constitutional crisis), produced the governors' options paper, and serves as the connective tissue between the doctrine layer (Chenoweth), the blocking coalition (UDA/NSL4A), and the constitutional reform program (NDC). RAD's recorded meetings span at least January 2026 through October 2026, indicating sustained operational continuity. RAD has no public website or formal incorporation visible in public records as of the date of this report.

United Democracy Alliance (UDA)

Mobilization Coordination · Blocking Coalition

UDA is described in the source transcripts as a large, deliberately low-profile coalition organized around four predefined red lines. It has no public website, no published membership roster, and no visible corporate or nonprofit filing as of October 2026. Paul Zeitz confirmed in recorded audio that "This is Erica Chenoweth's project, the UDA," with NSL4A (RADM Michael E. Smith) serving as the operationalization vehicle. Danielle Reiff described UDA as "a big, big, big, big, big tent" structured so that if any red line is crossed, "everybody's already pre-organized." The UDA appears to function as a commitment mechanism and mobilization trigger, rather than an ongoing operational body — its value is in the pre-coordination, not continuous activity.

Horizons Project

Bridge Layer Coordination · Network Weaving

The Horizons Project was founded by Julia Roig after she stepped down as president of PartnersGlobal (formerly Partners for Democratic Change), a USAID-funded international democracy-building NGO. The Horizons Project operates in the "Bridge" layer of the Block/Bridge/Build framework — focused on dialogue, narrative, and coalition building across civil society. Roig describes her role as "Chief Network Weaver." The project co-presents toolkit sessions with NSL4A (confirmed in NSL4A's October 14, 2026 YouTube session featuring both Smith and Roig), indicating structural integration with the blocking coalition despite its nominally different mission. The Horizons Project has a public profile in the conflict-resolution and democracy-reform sector.

Renew to Thrive (R2T)

Recording and Archiving Infrastructure · Inter-Movement Coordination

R2T is Walt Roberts' network, associated with the Mediators Foundation and the Inter-Movement Impact Project (IMIP). Its documented function in the Democracy Files is primarily operational: R2T organizes, records, and archives the Zoom sessions that became the leaked source material. Beyond archiving, Roberts and R2T participate in the broader inter-movement coordination work, connecting organizations across what Roberts describes as a need for better inter-movement collaboration. R2T is connected to both the Mediators Foundation (Roberts' registered nonprofit) and UnifyUSA (Zeitz), with Mediators Foundation listed as a current project partner for UnifyUSA on the Mediators Foundation's own website.

Blocking Coalition

National Security Leaders for America (NSL4A)

Blocking Coalition Operationalization · Security/Credentialing Layer

NSL4A is described in the transcripts as the vehicle through which UDA's blocking coalition is operationalized. Led by RADM Michael E. Smith (Ret.), NSL4A claimed approximately 1,600 former military officers, intelligence officials, and diplomats as of the recordings. NSL4A maintains a public presence including a YouTube channel (on which the October 14, 2026 toolkit session was posted) and a public advocacy role on national security matters. Within the Democracy Files network, NSL4A's specific function is scenario planning for emergency situations — including the October 14 session in which Smith describes an Insurrection Act table-top exercise with four scenarios, including one involving the use of ICE operations to provoke civil unrest.

Democracy Renewal Group (DRG)

Human Capital Pipeline · Foreign-Methodology Transfer

DRG consists of approximately 300 former USAID Democracy/Human Rights/Governance sector officers who organized following USAID's restructuring. The group's explicit mission, as documented in the April 2026 USAID Alumni Organization PDF, is to apply foreign democracy-assistance methodology domestically. Multiple Democracy Files participants — Reiff, Manton, Halloran, Adams, Carpenter, Mongan — are former USAID DRG-sector officers. DRG thus represents the human capital base from which the Democracy Files network draws its operational expertise: professionals who spent careers administering regime-transition and civil-society-building programs abroad and are now applying those skills to US domestic political organizing.

Federal Workers Against (Censorship/DOGE) (FWAD)

Federal Service Disruption · Blocking Layer

FWAD is described in the Democracy Files as "primarily a massive blocking organization" and a founding partner of the Nationwide Deliberation Campaign (NDC). Eric Manton's August 6 recorded message states that FWAD was "starting to consult federal workers on how to do some blocking inside the govt agencies during emergency situations." FWAD's resistance guide outlines tactics including malicious compliance, selective hearing, and hiding programs. The proposed Federal Service Protection Compact — a working paper drafted September 23, 2026 — would have governors provide formal cover and employment guarantees for federal workers engaged in nonviolent non-cooperation. Note: Two current federal workers named in the source files are not identified in this report and have been referred to appropriate agency contacts.

Street / Mobilization Layer

Indivisible

Mass Mobilization · Street Layer

Indivisible, co-founded and led by Ezra Levin, operates as one of the primary national mass-mobilization organizations in the Democracy Files ecosystem. Levin's recorded statement — "Erica, in many ways, has been the intellectual foundation for a lot of the strategy of the last 20 months" — directly links Indivisible's operational approach to Chenoweth's research. Indivisible has approximately 6,000 local chapters and has been the subject of mainstream press coverage of the anti-Trump protest movement. Its public profile, maintained by design, positions it as the visible face of a mobilization architecture whose strategic upper layers are not publicly disclosed.

50501 / No Kings

Protest Coordination · Street Layer

50501 and No Kings function as coordination brands for large-scale protest events rather than as formally incorporated organizations with defined leadership structures. 50501, which takes its name from "50 states, 50 protests, one day," organized simultaneous nationwide demonstrations. No Kings events mobilized larger crowds. Both are documented by InfluenceWatch and right-leaning media as embedded in a broader institutional infrastructure. Reiff describes them in Democracy Files audio as being like the UDA "on steroids" — signaling their role as the mass-mobilization surface expression of the more structured network operating above and behind them.

Beautiful Trouble

Direct Action Training · Street Layer

Beautiful Trouble is a direct action training and creative tactics organization founded and led by Nadine Bloch. It provides training in nonviolent civil disobedience, creative protest design, and direct action facilitation. In the Democracy Files' January 2026 forum, Bloch facilitated breakout sessions on emergency scenario responses. Her recorded statement — "there's not a clear possible transfer of power at the national level from the right to the left, given current circumstances" — frames her contribution as situational assessment for strategic planning, not merely protest training. Beautiful Trouble has an established public profile and has trained organizations across the progressive movement ecosystem.

Democracy Forward

Legal Strategy · Blocking Layer

Democracy Forward, led by Toby Payne, is the Democracy Files network's primary legal/litigation arm. The organization conducts legal challenges to executive actions and provides what the source files term "lawfare" support — a component of the "Block" pillar in the Block/Bridge/Build framework. Democracy Forward maintains a public profile as a progressive legal advocacy organization and has been active in challenging administrative actions across multiple federal agencies.

Build Layer — Constitutional Reform

Nationwide Deliberation Campaign (NDC)

Constitutional Reform Planning · Build Layer

The NDC, with FWAD as a founding partner, is the Democracy Files' primary "Build" layer initiative. Its mission is to produce a "People's Proposal" for structural constitutional reform, intended for adoption through what the source files describe as an "extra-constitutional process" — a Third Continental Congress or constitutional convention not authorized by existing Article V procedures. Paul Zeitz, who participates in both the NDC and RAD, frames this as a "Fourth Founding" moment. The NDC functions as the post-crisis governance architecture: if the "Block" layer's emergency scenarios materialize and are successfully navigated, the NDC is positioned to propose the replacement constitutional framework.

UnifyUSA

Constitutional Renewal Advocacy · Build Layer

UnifyUSA is Paul Zeitz's primary public-facing organization, described on the Mediators Foundation's website as a current project partner. Zeitz uses UnifyUSA's Substack and public platforms to advocate for constitutional renewal, framing the current political moment as requiring a "Third Founding" of American democracy. UnifyUSA's public messaging serves as the civic-engagement face of Zeitz's participation in the Democracy Files network's Build layer, providing a legitimate advocacy platform that contextualizes more private strategic planning activity.

International Solidarity Network

MEMO 98 (Slovakia)

Pre-Bunking / Disinformation Monitoring · International Layer

MEMO 98 is a Slovak NGO specializing in media monitoring and election observation. Founded in 1998 (the name references the Slovak elections of that year), it has conducted election monitoring across Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union and is affiliated with the European Platform for Democratic Elections. Rastislav Kuzel, its director, participated in the RAD International Solidarity session, contributing expertise in pre-bunking — proactive exposure of anticipated disinformation narratives before they circulate — drawn from MEMO 98's work in Slovakia and regional elections.

CRTA (Serbia)

Parallel Vote Tabulation / Polling Station Presence · International Layer

CRTA (Center for Research, Transparency and Accountability) is a Serbian civil society organization that conducted election monitoring during the contested 2023 Serbian elections. Its director, Rasa Nedeljkov, participated in the RAD International Solidarity session, offering methodology for parallel vote tabulation and rapid deployment of polling station monitors to detect and document irregularities. CRTA's experience in an environment where the ruling party used state resources for electoral advantage — a scenario structurally parallel to the Democracy Files' "election meltdown" scenario — was presented as directly applicable to US contingencies.

Transparencia Eleitoral Brasil

Post-Election Crisis Response / Situation Room Model · International Layer

Transparencia Eleitoral Brasil is a Brazilian election-monitoring NGO whose work during the 2022 Brazilian presidential election — particularly the post-election period when Jair Bolsonaro refused to concede — produced the "situation room" model cited by Ana Claudia Santano in the RAD International Solidarity session. The organization monitored electoral irregularities, documented challenges to the result, and coordinated with civil society to maintain public confidence in the electoral outcome. Its 2022 experience, in which a similar crisis was navigated, was presented as an operational template for potential US application.

Affiliated Support Organizations

PartnersGlobal (formerly Partners for Democratic Change)

Julia Roig's Former Organization · International Democracy-Building

PartnersGlobal is an international conflict resolution and civil society-building NGO with USAID funding history. Julia Roig served as its president before departing to found the Horizons Project. PartnersGlobal's mission — building democratic institutions and civil society capacity internationally — is the professional context from which Roig's domestic coordination work in the Democracy Files network derives. The Horizons Project remains listed on PartnersGlobal's website, suggesting organizational continuity.

Mediators Foundation

Walt Roberts' Registered Nonprofit · Coordination Infrastructure

The Mediators Foundation is Walt Roberts' registered nonprofit, based in Boulder, Colorado. It has received support from the Hewlett Foundation, giving it institutional standing in the progressive philanthropy ecosystem. The foundation hosts the Inter-Movement Impact Project and has listed UnifyUSA as a current project partner. It functions as the organizational shell through which Roberts conducts his coordination work and hosts the R2T meeting series whose recordings became the Democracy Files source material.

22nd Century Initiative (22CI)

Movement Strategy · Street/Strategy Interface

22CI is Scot Nakagawa's organization, focused on long-range progressive movement strategy. Nakagawa, who has written extensively on race, democracy, and movement building for Convergence magazine and other outlets, cited Chenoweth's 3.5% research in Democracy Files recordings and referenced No Kings protests as approaching that mobilization threshold. 22CI operates at the intersection of street mobilization and strategic planning, translating academic doctrine into organizing frameworks.

Persons of Interest

Profiles of named individuals documented in the Democracy Files source series. Note: Two current federal workers named in the source files are withheld from this report.

Doctrine Layer

Erica Chenoweth

Harvard Kennedy School / Radcliffe Institute · Network Intellectual Architect

Chenoweth is a political scientist at Harvard Kennedy School and the Radcliffe Institute, best known for developing the empirical case for nonviolent civil resistance in Why Civil Resistance Works (with Maria Stephan, 2011) and for the "3.5% rule" — the finding that nonviolent campaigns that mobilize 3.5% of a population have historically succeeded in producing political change. Multiple Democracy Files participants cite her research as the strategic foundation for their work. Paul Zeitz confirmed she is the founder of UDA. Ezra Levin called her "the intellectual foundation for a lot of the strategy of the last 20 months." Chenoweth co-leads Harvard's civil resistance research program with Zoe Marks. Her September 16, 2026 "Breakdown" panel at HKS with Steve Levitsky was publicly advertised.

Zoe Marks

Harvard Kennedy School · Research Collaborator

Marks is a faculty member at Harvard Kennedy School specializing in civil resistance, gender, and authoritarianism. She co-leads the Ash Center civil resistance research program with Chenoweth and has published on the relationship between misogyny and autocracy. Her research on how authoritarian movements target empowered women informs the Democracy Files network's framing of democratic backsliding. Within the network, Marks provides research collaboration and appears in recordings as a strategic adviser. Her HKS position provides academic credentialing for network activities.

Maria Stephan

Horizons Project / Former USIP · Nonviolent Strategy

Stephan is co-author of Why Civil Resistance Works with Chenoweth and a leading practitioner-scholar of nonviolent conflict. She previously served as a strategic adviser at the U.S. Institute of Peace (USIP) and has written for Just Security and other policy outlets. Stephan is affiliated with the Horizons Project and participates in the Democracy Files network in a dual capacity: as a doctrine co-author whose research legitimizes the network's framing, and as an active strategic participant. Her USIP background gives her connections within the national security policy community.

Coordination Layer

Danielle Reiff

Former USAID · RAD Co-Lead · Primary Operational Coordinator

Reiff is the most frequently documented operational coordinator across the Democracy Files recordings. A former USAID Democracy/Human Rights/Governance officer, she co-leads RAD with Paul Zeitz. Her recorded contributions span scenario planning (the three emergency scenarios), international outreach (soliciting guidance from MEMO 98, CRTA, Transparencia Eleitoral, and the Guatemalan constitutional lawyer), the governors' options paper, and the Insurrection Act table-top exercises. Her statement that the "meltdown scenario" represents a "window of opportunity" and her discussion of "extra-constitutional processes" are among the most consequential recorded content in the series. InfluenceWatch documents her professional background through the DemocracyAid organization listing.

Paul Zeitz

UnifyUSA / RAD Co-Lead · Constitutional Reform Architect

Zeitz co-leads RAD with Reiff and operates UnifyUSA as his public advocacy vehicle. His Substack frames the current moment as requiring a "Third Founding" and a "Fourth Founding Declaration." He confirmed in recordings that UDA is Chenoweth's project and described RAD's role as filling "the day after tomorrow" gap — preparing constitutional structures for the period after a crisis is successfully navigated. Zeitz has a background in global health advocacy (documented in past roles including at the Global AIDS Alliance) and brought those coalition-building skills to domestic political organizing. His NCDD affiliation confirms his integration into the deliberative democracy sector.

Julia Roig

Horizons Project · Network Coordinator / Bridge Layer Lead

Roig founded the Horizons Project after leading PartnersGlobal. She describes her role as "Chief Network Weaver" — a title that accurately reflects her function in the Democracy Files network: connecting organizations across the ecosystem, co-presenting toolkits with NSL4A, and maintaining the inter-organizational relationships that give the network its structural coherence. Her background in international conflict resolution and civil society development (documented through Beyond Intractability interviews and her academic affiliations at UC Berkeley and elsewhere) provides the professional legitimacy for her coordination role.

Walt Roberts

Mediators Foundation / R2T / IMIP · Recording Infrastructure Lead

Roberts leads the Mediators Foundation and the Inter-Movement Impact Project, both of which served as the organizational infrastructure hosting the Zoom sessions that became the Democracy Files source material. His Beyond Intractability interview discusses inter-movement coordination as a strategic imperative — the exact function R2T performs within the broader network. The Walter Roberts Endowment at George Washington University's Institute for Public Diplomacy and Global Communication (named for a different Walter Roberts — the former VOA director) is unrelated; this Roberts' professional background is in conflict resolution and philanthropically-supported movement coordination.

Security / Credentialing Layer

RADM Michael E. Smith (Ret.)

NSL4A · Blocking Coalition Operationalization

Rear Admiral Smith (Ret.) leads NSL4A, a coalition of approximately 1,600 former senior military officers, intelligence officials, and diplomats. Within the Democracy Files network, his role is twofold: providing the institutional credibility of a retired flag officer to the coalition's public advocacy, and operationalizing the blocking strategy through scenario planning. The October 14, 2026 NSL4A YouTube session — publicly posted — documented Smith facilitating Insurrection Act table-top exercises with four scenarios, including one involving the use of ICE operations to provoke civil unrest as a trigger event. His recorded "light bulb" moment — recognizing that RAD had "the day after tomorrow plan" — establishes his awareness of the full network architecture.

Former-Practitioner Layer (USAID/State)

Eric Manton

Former USAID · FWAD Participant

Manton is a former USAID Democracy/Human Rights/Governance officer and DRG member. His August 6 recorded message — "We are also starting to consult federal workers on how to do some blocking inside the govt agencies during emergency situations — but please do not put that on the form" — is among the most operationally specific statements in the source series. The explicit instruction not to record the consultation activity on official forms indicates awareness that the activity was sensitive. His participation in the Washington State Thriving Together forum confirms his active role in the network's regional outreach.

Brendan Halloran

Former USAID · DRG / Accountability Ecosystem Specialist

Halloran is a former USAID officer specializing in accountability ecosystems — the networks of civil society organizations, media, and institutions that constrain government power in fragile democracies. His professional publications (World Bank blog, AccountableNow, Open Government Partnership) document his work in applying these frameworks in international contexts. Within the Democracy Files network, he represents the professional expertise in civil society accountability mechanisms that the DRG is collectively applying domestically. His technical background in accountability ecosystem design is directly relevant to the network's strategy of building parallel accountability structures outside formal government channels.

Laura Adams

Former USAID · DRG Member

Adams is a former USAID Democracy/Human Rights/Governance officer with Central Asia and Eurasia regional expertise. Her professional background includes election monitoring and civil society development in post-Soviet states — the same regional context from which MEMO 98 and CRTA draw their operational models. Within the DRG and the Democracy Files network, she contributes expertise in how civil society organizations navigate state pressure in competitive authoritarian environments, directly applicable to the network's scenario planning.

Bryce Carpenter

Former USAID · DRG / NSL4A Member · Militarization Scenario Lead

Carpenter is a former USAID officer who is documented as both a DRG member and an NSL4A member — a dual affiliation that positions him at the intersection of the practitioner and security layers. He co-led the militarization scenario working group in the January 2026 RAD Forum, bringing operational expertise in how civil society organizations respond to military deployment in civilian contexts. His dual affiliation with both DRG and NSL4A makes him one of the clearest documented links between the two network layers.

John Mongan

Former USAID · DRG Member

Mongan is a former USAID DRG-sector officer and DRG member. His specific functional role within the Democracy Files network is less extensively documented in the source transcripts than those of Reiff, Manton, Halloran, and Carpenter, but his participation confirms the DRG's collective engagement with the RAD framework and scenario planning process.

Street / Mobilization Layer

Ezra Levin

Indivisible Co-Founder and CEO · Mass Mobilization

Levin co-founded Indivisible in 2016 following Trump's first election and has led it through its development into a ~6,000-chapter national network. His recorded statement crediting Chenoweth as "the intellectual foundation for a lot of the strategy of the last 20 months" is the most direct documented link between Indivisible's public mobilization activity and the Democracy Files network's strategic architecture. Levin maintains a public profile and has been the subject of mainstream press coverage of the anti-Trump resistance movement.

Nadine Bloch

Beautiful Trouble · Direct Action Training

Bloch is a direct action trainer, organizer, and co-editor of the Beautiful Trouble tactical handbook. She has trained organizations including IfNotNow, Bend the Arc, and others in nonviolent direct action. At the January 2026 RAD Forum, she facilitated breakout discussions on emergency scenario response. Her assessment — that there is no clear path for a "transfer of power at the national level from the right to the left, given current circumstances" — framed the strategic imperative behind the Democracy Files network's extra-electoral scenario planning approach.

Scot Nakagawa

22nd Century Initiative (22CI) · Movement Strategy

Nakagawa is a longtime progressive movement strategist and writer, formerly of the National Gay and Lesbian Task Force and other organizations, and currently associated with 22CI. He has written extensively on race, white nationalism, and democratic resilience for Convergence magazine. In Democracy Files recordings, he cited Chenoweth's 3.5% threshold and stated that No Kings protests were approaching that mobilization level — framing current protest activity as approaching the threshold for successful nonviolent campaign outcomes.

Evan Milligan

IMIP · Inter-Movement Coordination

Milligan is associated with the Inter-Movement Impact Project (IMIP), Roberts' coordination initiative. He appears in Democracy Files recordings discussing the 3.5% mobilization target and its strategic implications. His role within the network is coordination and movement strategy — helping translate the doctrine layer's research outputs into organizational strategy for the broader coalition.

Luke Pickrell

DSA / Marxist Unity Group Caucus · Ideological Left Edge

Pickrell is identified in the source transcripts as affiliated with the Democratic Socialists of America's Marxist Unity Group Caucus. His recorded statement — calling for an organization that is "explicitly anti-constitutional" — represents the ideological left boundary of the Democracy Files network. His presence in a RAD planning session indicates the network's deliberate inclusion of far-left elements alongside centrist and moderate-liberal participants, consistent with the UDA's described function as a "big tent" coalition that pre-organizes across a wide ideological range.

International Participants

Rastislav Kuzel

MEMO 98 (Slovakia) · Pre-Bunking and Media Monitoring

Kuzel directs MEMO 98, the Slovak media monitoring and election observation NGO. His contribution to the RAD International Solidarity session focused on pre-bunking methodology — proactively identifying and exposing anticipated disinformation narratives before they circulate widely, a technique MEMO 98 developed for Slovak and regional elections. His international standing within the European election monitoring community gives the RAD session's international dimension formal institutional grounding.

Rasa Nedeljkov

CRTA (Serbia) · Parallel Vote Tabulation and Election Monitoring

Nedeljkov leads CRTA, the Serbian civil society organization that monitored the 2023 Serbian elections under conditions of alleged state interference. His contribution to the RAD International Solidarity session covered parallel vote tabulation methodology and rapid polling station deployment — techniques developed to detect and document electoral fraud in real time. His experience in an environment where the ruling apparatus used state resources against the opposition provides operational models directly applicable to the Democracy Files' "election meltdown" scenario.

Ana Claudia Santano

Transparencia Eleitoral Brasil · Post-Election Crisis Response

Santano is associated with Transparencia Eleitoral Brasil and contributed expertise on the organization's "situation room" model, developed during the 2022 Brazilian presidential election's post-election crisis. The situation room concept — a coordinated civil society crisis response center that monitors, documents, and responds to electoral irregularities in real time — was explicitly cited by Reiff as a model for potential US application. Santano's participation represents the Democracy Files network's most direct operational borrowing from a recent democratic-resilience success story.

Edgar Ortiz Romero

Guatemala · Constitutional Litigation and Mobilization

Ortiz Romero is a Guatemalan constitutional lawyer who contributed to the RAD International Solidarity session. Guatemala's 2023 presidential election, in which the attorney general attempted to overturn the results, was successfully defended through a combination of constitutional litigation and civil society mobilization — the Block layer's legal-plus-street strategy in direct application. Ortiz Romero's contribution provided operational detail on how that defense was executed, directly relevant to the Democracy Files' election meltdown scenario planning.

Melissa Hooper

RAD International Solidarity Working Group Lead · International Law

Hooper leads RAD's International Solidarity Working Group, which organized the session documented in Democracy Files Audio 3. Her professional background is in international human rights law, with affiliations at the German Marshall Fund, Stanford Law School, and UC College of the Law San Francisco. She previously directed Human Rights First's human rights and civil society program. Her role within the Democracy Files network is to integrate international expertise and solidarity into the domestic strategic planning process — formalizing the lesson-borrowing from Eastern European and Latin American democratic resilience experiences that characterizes the network's international engagement.
