November Outages, Federal Breaches and Typhoon Linkages: Extended Update

BLUF

No reporting ties any November outage or federal breach to a Chinese Typhoon hacking group. A new breach of unclassified CIA, ODNI and NGA systems surfaced on 24 Nov with no named actor, and officials continue to attribute the outages to software patching, not attack.

Change Since Last Report

New since 23 Nov: the CIA, ODNI and NGA breach; Bessent’s patching explanation; JPMorgan’s vendor breach; an FBI water-sector warning; and the White House’s statement that Trump will nominate Davies, with Clayton as interim cyber director. The assessment has not shifted; nothing supports a Typhoon link.

Key Facts

  • Intelligence breach. The New York Times reported on 24 Nov a breach of an unclassified CIA and ODNI network for open-source intelligence. ODNI said critical capabilities and classified systems were not disrupted. AP, citing the Times, says the NGA was affected (Reuters; CNN; AP).
  • ARTEX. Nextgov reports, via AP, that the intrusion tool ARTEX is believed used; officials have not confirmed it. The FBI’s 13 Nov alert calls ARTEX an open-source tool that drives AI models and attributes no campaign to a state or group (AP; FBI).
  • ARTEX use. The FBI says attackers have used ARTEX since August against financial, government, technology, defense and energy targets, including South Korean financial institutions and customers of ConnectWise Cloud Backup. It says the tool lets low-skilled actors run complex intrusions (FBI).
  • Cyber director. The White House named Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon’s chief information officer, on 19 Nov to lead its cyber office after Cairncross left. On 23 Nov the White House said Trump will nominate her, with Clayton as interim director (White House; Nextgov; Anadolu).
  • Treasury Secretary. On 22 Nov Bessent told CBS banks are “by far the most resilient companies in the U.S. economy” and tied bank outages to patching security systems for AI-related threats, likening the disruption to a vaccine’s side effects (Epoch Times; The Hill).
  • JPMorgan Chase. On 19 Nov JPMorgan said hackers hit third-party vendors and may have accessed some customer names and email addresses from marketing data. It reported no service disruption and said the attack was unrelated to its online banking outage (Reuters; Epoch Times).
  • Clayton’s statements. On ABC on 22 Nov Clayton said the incidents were not coordinated or state-sponsored and pointed to dated digital systems. On CNN he told Americans to expect more glitches and interruptions as organizations modernize (ABC News; Nextgov).
  • Patch volume. Qualys counted more than 1,200 Microsoft vulnerability fixes in November, nearly 20 times last November’s total. VulnCheck counts about 100,000 disclosed vulnerabilities in 2026. Experts told Reuters that untested emergency patches can themselves cause failures (Reuters via BNN Bloomberg).
  • NCIC and USDA. The FBI declined to comment on the NCIC intrusion, and no actor is public. The Agriculture Department took numerous applications offline, including farmer payment tools, and reset all database passwords; no actor or data loss is known (CNN; Nextgov).
  • Earlier federal breaches. In September the FBI told employees its jobs portal was breached, and a threat actor said it would publish stolen data. The Pentagon’s personnel-records center disclosed a breach of roughly 3 million people, undetected for about nine months (ABC7 Chicago; Nextgov).
  • Water warning. The Hill’s headline reports an FBI warning that threats to water systems are rising; its text was not available. On 30 Jul the FBI and EPA said utilities in about seven states had reported incidents degrading operations (The Hill; NBC New York).
  • Water attribution. The July notice names no country or group (Foley Hoag). A law enforcement official told NBC the Minnesota attacks on more than 30 water systems had hallmarks of Iranian activity, and the investigation continues (NBC New York; FOX 5 DC).
  • Other outages. Delta Air Lines said an automated weight-and-balance problem delayed Atlanta flights on 22 Nov and it used manual workarounds. Microsoft said its 12 Nov outage was a months-old glitch combined with other factors, not a cyberattack (Epoch Times).
  • Typhoon status. Reuters places the CIA breach among the USDA, two FBI, Pentagon and Homeland Security incidents without naming an actor. No outlet or agency has tied a November incident to a Typhoon group (Reuters; FBI).

Points of Dispute

Whether patching explains the outages. Bessent and Clayton attribute the disruptions to patching and technical changes, and Clayton says they were neither coordinated nor state-sponsored (Epoch Times; ABC News). Reuters’s expert sources say the surge in emergency patches can itself cause failures (Reuters via BNN Bloomberg). Companies give separate causes: Microsoft a months-old glitch, the FAA a router outage (Epoch Times).

Who is behind the federal breaches, and whether China is involved. Clayton declined to discuss the FBI and USDA incidents (Daily Caller). Reuters lists the incidents together without naming an actor for the CIA breach, and no source collected names one for NCIC or USDA (Reuters). Dream found a China-nexus cluster exploiting web-server flaws called PHP2Shell and Aviatrix says the activity mirrors Typhoon patterns; Dream names no group (Dream; Aviatrix).

Whether ARTEX points to a state actor. Nextgov, via AP, says ARTEX is believed to have been used against the CIA, unconfirmed (AP via GSM Global News). The FBI says malicious actors use ARTEX, that it lowers the skill needed for complex intrusions, and that it attributes no campaign to a state or group (FBI).

Whether the cyber director’s status is settled. Epoch Times reported that the White House had not said whether Davies is acting director or a nominee (Epoch Times). A White House official told Nextgov on 23 Nov that Trump will nominate her and Clayton leads in the interim, which settles the question; Senate action remains (Nextgov).

Who is behind the water-sector activity. A law enforcement official told NBC the Minnesota attacks bore hallmarks of Iranian activity (NBC New York). The FBI and EPA notice names no country or group, and state and local officials said water safety was not threatened (Foley Hoag; FOX 5 DC).

Background

Outages began on 12 Nov and hit Microsoft 365, FAA air traffic control, major banks, United Airlines and NSANet, the NSA’s classified network (AP; Newsweek; Al Jazeera). On 20 Nov CNN reported an FBI investigation of an intrusion into the National Crime Information Center, its criminal-records database (CNN). The Agriculture Department disclosed a breach on 16 Nov (Nextgov). PHP2Shell is a set of web-server flaws that the NSA urged users to patch (TweakTown). “Typhoon” is the label American security firms give Chinese state-linked hacking groups. Volt Typhoon hides access in critical-infrastructure networks for use in a crisis, Salt Typhoon breached telecommunications carriers, Flax Typhoon runs botnets and Silk Typhoon exploits IT suppliers. Such access shows no effect until used, so a linkage needs a matching sector, access path, attribution and timing. No source supplies those for the November incidents. The AI-patching explanation traces to AI models that find software flaws quickly: on 07 Apr Bessent and then-Federal Reserve Chair Powell convened bank chief executives over Anthropic’s Claude Mythos Preview (Fox News). Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude, the model that produced this report.
