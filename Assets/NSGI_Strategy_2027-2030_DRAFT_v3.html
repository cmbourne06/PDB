NATIONAL SECURITY AND GOVERNMENT INSTITUTE

NSGI STRATEGY, 2027–2030

Lines of Effort, Phasing, Resource Model, and Measures

DRAFT FOR EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR REVIEW | November 12, 2026

Prepared in support of the NSGI Executive Director by Christopher M. Bourne, Bourne Executive Strategies LLC

SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND BOTTOM LINE

1.1. PURPOSE.

This document sets out the National Security and Government Institute (NSGI) strategy for 2027 through 2030, with detailed planning through 2028. It translates the Chancellor's guidance into a mission, intent, lines of effort, phasing, resource model, and measures, and identifies the decisions required to begin execution. Execution begins 1 January 2027. Dates refer to calendar years; "FY" designations refer only to items driven by the federal budget cycle (federal fiscal year, 1 October–30 September).

1.2. BOTTOM LINE.

(1) NSGI is organized along four lines of effort (LOEs). LOE 1, Cyber and Infrastructure Security, and LOE 2, Executive Education and Public Engagement, are the decisive lines; they define the Institute and carry the Chancellor's two stated priorities. LOE 3, Federal Market Access and the Defense Industrial Base, and LOE 4, Security Workforce Development, are supporting lines. They give Upstate executives a practical reason to engage with NSGI, supply talent to regional employers, and generate revenue and grant funding.

(2) LOE 2 is the main effort in Phase I (2027) because NSGI controls it and it produces revenue in the current year. The main effort transitions to LOE 1 when external funding for cyber and infrastructure security is secured or the dedicated cybersecurity facility is approved, anticipated during 2028.

(3) The revenue objective is to cover expenses. Each fee-based offering breaks even on direct costs in 2027; earned revenue covers all NSGI non-personnel operating expenses by the end of 2028; and earned revenue and grants fund growth positions in 2029–2030. The plan assumes the University continues to fund the two current positions.

(4) Execution depends on five Chancellor decisions in Phase I, the most important being authority for NSGI to retain the fees, memberships, and sponsorships it earns (Section 9).

SECTION 2: STRATEGIC CONTEXT

2.1. CHANCELLOR'S GUIDANCE.

The Chancellor has directed that NSGI continue its focus on cybersecurity and executive education, generate revenue sufficient to cover its expenses, and operate at current staffing with growth as funding allows. This strategy adds infrastructure security as an extension of the cybersecurity line, tied to the region's nuclear and energy base, and adds a workforce line in which NSGI convenes technical colleges, innovation centers, high school programs, and regional employers. Both additions serve the two priorities the Chancellor set.

2.2. CURRENT SITUATION.

a. Strengths.

(1) NSGI originated and coordinates the four-campus USC System Integrated Cyber Program Team (ICPT). USC System President Amiridis ranked NSGI's FY25 cybersecurity request second and its FY26 request sixth among System congressional priorities.

(2) The Institute has an established working relationship with Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL), an open state-funding discussion with the South Carolina Research Authority (SCRA), and a national-level network of senior defense, government, and industry practitioners.

(3) Executive education products, pricing, and break-even models exist: the Global Engagement Certificate, half-day Spotlight seminars ($350 per day), and a two-day executive package ($1,500).

(4) Student-facing programs have a track record: Cyber Camps (2025, 2026), the Clemson AFROTC crosstown pathway, AFJROTC outreach, internships, and Aviation & Aerospace Day.

(5) The University Center of Greenville location places NSGI within reach of the Upstate's executive community.

b. Limitations.

(1) The $50,000 seed allocation in 2023 is the only designated operating funding NSGI has received. No congressional, philanthropic, or sustained earned revenue has been realized. Program expenses are approved case by case by the Chancellor.

(2) Staff consists of the Executive Director and the Marketing Coordinator.

(3) Three congressional requests (FY25–FY27) went unfunded; the FY28 request uses the same mechanism.

(4) Announced partnerships with U.S. Army Cyber Command and the SC National Guard 125th Cyber Protection Battalion have not been activated.

(5) The public website presents NSGI as a speaker and executive education program, lists no events after April 2026, and does not describe the cyber, workforce, or industry work.

2.3. OPERATING ENVIRONMENT.

a. State Landscape.

Other South Carolina institutions have claimed distinct security lanes: Clemson's National Security Institute (launched March 2026) in sponsored defense research; USC Aiken in operational cyber, anchored by the National Guard cyber facility and SRNL; USC Beaufort in maritime and port cybersecurity; The Citadel in the cadet-to-DoD cyber pipeline; and SC State in the Intelligence Community diversity pipeline. SCRA launched the state's Center for Cybersecurity in December 2025. No institution owns the intersection of the Upstate defense industrial base, regional infrastructure security, and executive-level national security education. That lane is NSGI's to claim.

b. Federal Policy.

The Department of War suspended Phase 2 of the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) program on 13 July 2026, and a 3 September 2026 class deviation made the suspension binding on contracting officers. Phase 1 self-assessment requirements remain, and prime contractors may still impose their own requirements on suppliers. Two implications follow: NSGI's business offerings lead with market access and supply chain security and treat CMMC as one compliance topic among several; and CMMC references in the FY28 congressional request require revision before filing.

c. Regional Industrial Base.

The Upstate's national security footprint is industrial: Lockheed Martin's F-16 production line in Greenville and its supplier base, GE Aerospace and GE Vernova, BMW and its tier suppliers, Duke Energy's Oconee Nuclear Station, and Inland Port Greer. These firms carry export-control, supply chain, controlled unclassified information, and operational technology security obligations that no Upstate institution addresses as a regional service.

d. Training and Innovation Centers.

The Upstate's technical training capacity is distributed across many institutions: Greenville Technical College's Center for Manufacturing Innovation; Greenville County Schools' CTE Innovation Center at Roper Mountain, which includes networking and cybersecurity; Daniel Morgan Technology Center and R.D. Anderson Applied Technology Center in Spartanburg County; Spartanburg Community College (SCC), an NSA-designated National Center of Academic Excellence in Cyber Defense, including its Tyger River Spark Center and Cherokee County manufacturing campus; Tri-County Technical College, which serves Oconee County; Piedmont Technical College's O'Dell Center; BMW's training center; and Clemson's CU-ICAR. No one connects these institutions to national security employers. That gap defines NSGI's convening role in LOE 4.

2.4. CENTER OF GRAVITY AND CRITICAL VULNERABILITY.

NSGI's center of gravity is its convening credibility: access to senior national security practitioners, the Chancellor's sponsorship, and a Greenville presence. Its critical requirements are continued Chancellor sponsorship, an active practitioner network, and recurring products that executives and employers will pay for. Its critical vulnerability is a two-person staff without confirmed authority to retain earned revenue. The strategy protects the center of gravity by tying every activity to a product or partnership, and closes the vulnerability through the revenue decision in Section 9 and partner-delivered programs that keep the staff load within capacity.

SECTION 3: MISSION, INTENT, AND END STATE

3.1. MISSION.

NSGI educates Upstate executives and public leaders on national security, builds cybersecurity and infrastructure security capacity at USC Upstate and across the region, and connects Upstate businesses and workforce institutions with federal markets and security employers, in order to strengthen the region's defense industrial base and critical infrastructure and to generate the visibility and revenue that sustain the Institute and advance USC Upstate.

3.2. INTENT.

a. Purpose.

Establish NSGI as the Upstate's recognized institution for national security education and for cyber and infrastructure security, funded by a diversified and self-sustaining revenue base.

b. Method.

The four lines reinforce one another. LOE 3 gives executives a business reason to engage and becomes the audience and source of content for LOE 2. LOE 4 supplies talent to LOE 3 businesses and to LOE 1 employers and programs. LOE 1 gives LOE 2 its substance and credibility. LOE 2 converts participants into council members, sponsors, internship hosts, and employers for LOEs 3 and 4. NSGI acts as convener and broker. Partners deliver services they already provide, and NSGI adds senior-level access, national security content, and coordination.

c. End State (December 2030).

(1) Earned revenue and grants cover all NSGI program and operating expenses and fund at least one position beyond the two currently supported by the University.

(2) No single funding source exceeds 40 percent of NSGI resources.

(3) USC Upstate's dedicated cybersecurity education, laboratory, and cyber-range facility is operating, and NSGI leads an infrastructure security program with energy, nuclear, and utility partners.

(4) NSGI chairs a standing council of Upstate defense and federal businesses and convenes a standing workforce partnership with tracked placements.

(5) NSGI publishes an annual calendar of executive education offerings and hosts a recognized annual Upstate national security forum.

3.3. PLANNING ASSUMPTIONS.

(1) The University continues to fund the Executive Director and Marketing Coordinator positions.

(2) No new designated operating allocation is provided; growth is funded from earned revenue, grants, and awards.

(3) Congressional funding remains uncertain. The strategy succeeds without it and accelerates with it.

(4) The strategy remains executable through any leadership transition the Chancellor directs.

(5) SC APEX Accelerator, the SC Manufacturing Extension Partnership (SCMEP), and the technical colleges will collaborate under memoranda of understanding.

SECTION 4: LINES OF EFFORT

4.1. LOE 1: CYBER AND INFRASTRUCTURE SECURITY.

a. Objective (End of 2028).

NSGI leads USC Upstate's role in the USC System cyber enterprise; the dedicated cybersecurity facility is funded or approved; and an Upstate infrastructure security program operates with energy, nuclear, and utility partners.

b. Ways.

(1) USC System cyber enterprise. Continue coordinating the ICPT. Coordinate the FY28 request with the other campuses at the January 2027 ICPT meeting, and secure Upstate's role as defined in the FY28 proposal: ICPT coordination, a regional defense-supplier cyber clinic, undergraduate workforce growth, and the cyber range.

(2) Funding portfolio. Pursue four sources in parallel:

(a) the FY28 congressional request ($9.6 million, RDT&E Defense-Wide, PE 0606771D8Z), with revised CMMC language;

(b) the SCRA state pathway transmitted on 27 October 2026;

(c) NSF proposals coordinated through the ICPT (the CyberAICorps Scholarship for Service with USC Beaufort in spring 2027 and a System EPSCoR proposal in calendar 2027);

(d) workforce subawards under the regional grid and energy initiatives with Upstate scope.

(3) Dedicated cybersecurity facility. Support the Chancellor's priority to move cyber instruction out of the Hodge into dedicated classroom, laboratory, and cyber-range space. Develop state capital and philanthropic options in parallel with the congressional request.

(4) Infrastructure security program. Extend cybersecurity into the operational technology that runs power generation, the grid, water, and manufacturing. Anchor partners: Duke Energy (Oconee Nuclear Station), GE Vernova, Upstate utilities and municipalities, and SRNL. Products: a critical infrastructure tabletop exercise series for operators and local government; applied student projects; and extension of the SRNL cooperative research agreement to USC Upstate, pending the System's determination of its scope.

(5) Military partnerships. Convert the 228th Theater Tactical Signal Brigade relationship into recurring participation in exercises, curriculum review, and speaking. Open formal engagement with the 125th Cyber Protection Battalion and U.S. Army Cyber Command.

(6) Naval Information Warfare Center (NIWC) Atlantic. Reengage and define the relationship. It dates to early 2024 and rests on individual contacts rather than an agreement: Erik Gardner of NIWC Atlantic and the National Security Innovation Network (NSIN), a February 2024 campus visit that explored the cyber lab, student engagement, boot camps, and certificates, a February 2025 discussion with John Garstka and Dr. Mike Matthews, and academic engagement by Dr. Jeannie Chapman. No agreement or funded project has resulted. Confirm current status with the Executive Director and convert the relationship into one defined project, such as student work through NIWC Atlantic's Palmetto Tech Bridge, which the FY28 request cites.

4.2. LOE 2: EXECUTIVE EDUCATION AND PUBLIC ENGAGEMENT.

a. Objective (End of 2028).

NSGI publishes a fixed annual calendar of fee-based or sponsored offerings that draw their content from LOEs 1 and 3, hosts a flagship annual forum, and every offering meets or exceeds break-even.

b. Ways.

(1) Restructure the Global Engagement Certificate into three tracks.

(a) National Security and the Upstate Economy: the defense industrial base, F-16 production and foreign military sales, export controls, supply chain and foreign-ownership risk, and strategic competition with China.

(b) Cyber and Infrastructure Risk for Executives and Boards: governance of cyber and operational technology risk and incident decision-making, built around tabletop exercises.

(c) Global Engagement: the existing geopolitics, diplomacy, and leadership courses.

(2) Publish an annual calendar twelve months in advance. Deliver at least two offerings in the second half of 2027 and at least four offerings plus the flagship forum in 2028.

(3) Establish an annual Upstate National Security Forum, funded by sponsorship, that showcases council members, partners, and NSGI programs.

(4) Tie each speaker event to a track or a council meeting so that no event stands alone without a revenue or partnership purpose.

(5) Use public engagement as the marketing channel. Use the podcast, FOX Carolina interviews, and a rebuilt website with a current events calendar to drive enrollment and membership.

(6) Price at break-even plus margin, with discounts for council members. Break-even means fee and sponsorship revenue equal to or greater than the offering's direct costs: instructor and speaker honoraria, travel, venue, catering, materials, and marketing. The pricing baseline is NSGI's August 2025 course break-even workbook, cited in Section 18 of the Continuity Book, which models course pricing, enrollment, instructor cost, and break-even enrollment. That workbook also carries a $252,000 fully loaded personnel envelope. Update it in early 2027 to price against direct costs only, since the University funds the two current positions.

4.3. LOE 3: FEDERAL MARKET ACCESS AND THE DEFENSE INDUSTRIAL BASE.

a. Objective (End of 2028).

NSGI chairs a standing Upstate Defense and Federal Business Council that links prime contractors, federal agencies, and regional suppliers and is sustained by membership and sponsorship.

b. Ways.

(1) Upstate Defense and Federal Business Council. Members: prime contractors (Lockheed Martin Greenville, GE Aerospace, GE Vernova), tier suppliers, and defense-relevant firms in the automotive supplier base. Partner organizations sit ex officio without fees: SC APEX Accelerator, SCMEP, the SC Small Business Development Centers, SC Department of Commerce, Upstate SC Alliance, and the Greenville and Spartanburg chambers. Corporate members pay tiered dues.

(2) Quarterly "Doing Business With" forums featuring small-business and supply chain liaisons from federal buyers and primes, such as the Defense Logistics Agency, Army and Navy contracting activities, NIWC Atlantic, the General Services Administration (GSA), the Department of Energy and SRNL, and Lockheed Martin supply chain. Each forum includes a matchmaking session.

(3) Broker role. Refer firms to SC APEX Accelerator for no-cost contracting counseling, including System for Award Management (SAM) registration, GSA Multiple Award Schedule preparation, and proposal support, and to SCMEP for cybersecurity and operational assistance. NSGI adds demand-side access and senior relationships. Formalize the referral arrangements by MOU.

(4) Compliance and security workshops on export controls (ITAR and EAR), protection of controlled unclassified information (NIST SP 800-171, including CMMC status), and supply chain and foreign-ownership risk.

(5) Annual Upstate Defense Industrial Base Assessment (Phase III). A student-staffed product mapping regional capability and risk that feeds LOE 2 content and congressional engagement.

4.4. LOE 4: SECURITY WORKFORCE DEVELOPMENT.

a. Objective (End of 2028).

NSGI convenes a standing Upstate Security Workforce Partnership that links high school programs, technical colleges, innovation centers, USC Upstate, and employers into tracked pathways for security-sensitive occupations.

b. Ways.

(1) Convene the partnership. Technical colleges: SCC (anchor; NSA Center of Academic Excellence in Cyber Defense), Greenville Technical College, Tri-County Technical College, and Piedmont Technical College. Innovation and career centers: Roper Mountain CTE Innovation Center, Daniel Morgan Technology Center, R.D. Anderson Applied Technology Center, and SCC's Spark Center. Employers from the LOE 3 council and LOE 1 partners.

(2) Build the pathway from high school (Cyber Camps expanded to a year-round track; CTE cybersecurity programs) to a technical college credential, a USC Upstate degree, and employment. Use joint credentials, with employers defining the requirements.

(3) Match talent to employers. Broker internships and apprenticeships with council employers; embed a clearance-readiness module in technical college programs; participate in the ICPT government job fair; and track placements.

(4) Integrate military and veterans. Arrange DoD SkillBridge placements for transitioning service members with council employers; partner with Upstate Warrior Solutions and the USC Upstate Veterans Lounge; and use the Clemson AFROTC crosstown agreement and Presbyterian College Army ROTC as pathways into cyber and acquisition careers.

(5) Fund through grants and sponsorship: the NSF CyberAICorps proposal with USC Beaufort, U.S. Department of Labor and SC Department of Employment and Workforce grants, and employer sponsorship of camps and scholarships.

SECTION 6: RESOURCE MODEL

6.1. FUNDING PRINCIPLES.

(1) Maintain several independent funding channels; no single source exceeds 40 percent of NSGI resources by 2030.

(2) Account separately for unrestricted earned revenue and restricted external awards. Restricted funds build capability; unrestricted revenue sustains operations.

(3) Record requests, commitments, awards, and receipts separately, as the Continuity Book recommends.

(4) Price every fee-based offering to cover its direct costs before launch.

6.2. REVENUE OBJECTIVE.

"Cover expenses" is defined in three stages. Phase I: each fee-based offering breaks even on direct costs. Phase II: earned revenue covers all NSGI non-personnel operating expenses (events, travel, marketing, materials), ending case-by-case program funding requests. Phase III: earned revenue and grants fund at least one additional position.

Phases I and II run on the current staff, augmented by student interns, the Executive in Residence, advisory council volunteers, and partner organizations that deliver services under MOU. SECTION 9: COORDINATION AND DECISIONS

9.1. INTERNAL COORDINATION.

Execution requires coordination with the Provost and Deans (academic credit for internships, faculty participation in tracks), the USC Upstate Cybersecurity Lab, the Greenville campus, the USC Upstate Foundation, sponsored programs, and, at the System level, the ICPT and the USC System Washington office.

9.2. DECISIONS REQUESTED OF THE CHANCELLOR.

a. Revenue Retention.

Authorize an NSGI account that retains executive education fees, council dues, and sponsorships for NSGI operations.

b. Strategy Approval.

Approve the four lines of effort and the phased main effort.

c. Phase I Market Test.

Approve the pilot council and the Track (a) and Track (b) pilot courses in 2027.

d. Institutional Roles.

Designate NSGI as the USC Upstate lead for the infrastructure security program and for convening the Upstate Security Workforce Partnership.

e. FY28 Congressional Request.

Endorse revision of CMMC references in the FY28 request to reflect the July 2026 suspension.

9.3. NEXT STEPS.

Executive Director review and revision; brief to the Chancellor; on approval, begin Phase I actions in Table 3.
