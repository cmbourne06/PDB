US Blockade of Iran Continues as Trump Meets Qatar’s Prime Minister

The US naval blockade of Iran at the Strait of Hormuz continues, and President Trump said on 09 Nov, after meeting Qatar’s prime minister, that he does not expect a renewed offensive. Iran’s security council head denies that negotiations are under way.

Key Facts

  • Trump called the blockade the greatest in history and said a new CENTCOM task force is operating against Iran (The Hill via Yahoo News).
  • CENTCOM announced Task Force Iron Gate on 07 Nov to extend the blockade to land borders and the Caspian Sea, and reports 147 vessels redirected since July (Fox News).
  • Trump tied the absence of new attacks to Iran signing a strong nuclear deal and reopening Hormuz (Fox News).
  • Iranian President Pezeshkian is described as open to talks (Breitbart).
  • After the meeting Trump signaled positive momentum with Iran (Al Jazeera) and said Iran is effectively finished militarily (Breitbart).

Points of Dispute

Whether negotiations exist. Iran’s security council head Mohsen Rezaei dismisses reports of talks and sets seven conditions, including sanctions relief and an end to the blockade; Iran’s foreign minister says a two-day proposal stands and midterm results stalled mediators. Secretary Rubio says no breakthrough is imminent (Breitbart; Fox News).

Reported terms. Axios, as cited by Breitbart, reported a sequence in which the US eases some restrictions, then Iran reopens Hormuz and resumes nuclear talks. A senior Qatari official called the reported terms “categorically false” (Breitbart).

Watch

  2. Does Iran answer the Qatar-mediated proposal, or does Task Force Iron Gate widen the blockade? Assessment: A signed US–Iran nuclear agreement by 30 Nov is very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on Rezaei’s seven conditions and Rubio’s remarks.
  3. Does Europe gain a seat in the Abu Dhabi room? Germany’s foreign minister wants one; Russia insists the format is trilateral (Al Jazeera). Assessment: A formal European seat this round is very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on Russia’s insistence on a trilateral format.
