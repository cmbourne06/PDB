Iran and U.S. Exchange Fire in the Gulf; Tankers Reported Hit by Mines in Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it launched drones at a U.S.-operated radar in Bahrain, and a U.S. official says U.S. forces struck a missile launch site on Qeshm island in response. Kuwait separately reported intercepting hostile drones.

Change Since Last Report

Since the 16 Nov report on Bahrain and the third U.S. carrier: a U.S. strike on Qeshm island, drone interceptions in Kuwait, and three tankers reported hit by mines.

Key Facts

  • CENTCOM said there was no damage to U.S. equipment or personnel, and Bahrain said it intercepted the drones aimed at Sheikh Isa air base (Al Jazeera).
  • The U.S. official said the Qeshm strike came "in response to IRGC aggression targeting U.S. personnel and assets"; Iran's Hormozgan deputy governor said it caused no residential or infrastructure damage (Epoch Times; Al Jazeera).
  • AFP calls Bahrain the third Iranian drone target in five days and the first Gulf state hit since early September (Al Jazeera).
  • Iran's armed forces said three tankers hit mines in Hormuz on 17 Nov; UKMTO says the Tara Gas was hit twice on 16 Nov, with crew unaccounted for (Al Jazeera; TASS).

Points of Dispute

Cause and target. The Revolutionary Guard says it attacked the radar, cites U.S. drone incursions, and claims it downed a U.S. MQ-1 over Qeshm. The War Department calls the Qeshm strike self-defense, and CENTCOM says all drones were intercepted (Epoch Times).

Hormuz mines. Iran says tankers hit mines, and the Guard says the gas carrier used a route Iran had not authorized. The United States says the main routes are cleared (Al Jazeera).

  2. Does CENTCOM or UKMTO attribute the 16 Nov tanker strike to Iran by 23 Nov? Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on UKMTO reporting the hits without naming a source.
  3. Is a signed U.S.–Iran deal announced by 30 Nov? Assessment: Very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on the exchange of fire; Iran and Oman discussed shipping safety in Muscat, but no deal is reported.

Transportation Department Requests $50.1 Billion for Air Traffic Control; Senators Seek Pause on Router Upgrades

The Transportation Department asked appropriators for $50.1 billion for air traffic control, and lawmakers of both parties doubt the request ahead of the 11 Dec funding deadline. Democrats say last year's $12.5 billion authorization was largely unspent.

Change Since Last Report

The 17 Nov report covered Sen. Thune's objection to a $50 billion request. New: the formal $50.1 billion request, a 13-senator letter, and the FAA's pause on router installations.

Key Facts

  • Transportation Secretary Duffy's department made the request on 17 Nov; he has pressed Congress more than 30 times in two years (Daily Signal).
  • Duffy said he will take what he can get, and Rep. Ocasio-Cortez opposed what she called a blank check (The Hill; The Hill).
  • Thirteen Democratic senators led by Sen. Cantwell asked the FAA to pause replacing more than 3,000 routers, and the FAA paused installations (Al Jazeera; Epoch Times).
  • L3Harris says 3,648 routers are slated for replacement, about 400 are installed and 69 are operational (Epoch Times).

Points of Dispute

Need for the money. The administration says the air traffic control system needs about $50 billion. Democrats say it was authorized $12.5 billion last year, used a fraction, and should present a plan before receiving more (Al Jazeera).

Watch

  1. Is a funding bill enacted by 11 Dec without a lapse? Assessment: A lapse is unlikely (low confidence), based on 23 days remaining and no bill text yet.

Commerce Rule Limits Chinese Remote Access to AI Chips; Treasury and Commerce Split Over Nvidia

A Commerce interim final rule, drafted at Treasury and National Security Council direction, restricts Chinese firms' remote access to advanced U.S. AI chips in overseas data centers from 19 Nov. Treasury and Commerce differ on enforcement, and a Senate hearing is set for 03 Dec.

Change Since Last Report

The 13 Nov report covered Huang's resignation as CEO. New: the rule, a 03 Dec Senate hearing, and the board chair's statement that it does not intend to reinstate Huang.

Key Facts

  • The Bureau of Industry and Security issued the rule on 16 Nov; it extends protections on advanced chip shipments to remote access through data centers in third countries (BIS).
  • Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103 percent, and guided to about $130 billion for the fourth quarter (Al Jazeera).
  • Nvidia's board formed a national security and export control committee; board chair Jones said it does not intend to reinstate Huang, and Colette Kress is interim CEO (Fox News; Al Jazeera).
  • Al Jazeera reports Lutnick and Huang are summoned to testify before the Senate on 03 Dec without naming the committee; The Hill's headline says Huang agreed to testify before Senate Banking (Al Jazeera; The Hill).
  • China's Foreign Ministry called the rule economic coercion and promised countermeasures (Fox News).

Points of Dispute

Enforcement. Treasury Secretary Bessent criticized Commerce Secretary Lutnick for delaying action on Megaspeed, a Singapore firm accused of diverting Nvidia chips, and wants stricter enforcement of existing rules. Lutnick backs Nvidia sales to China. Trump calls it a difference in approach and says he will not remove Lutnick (Fox News; Al Jazeera).

Watch

  1. Do Huang or Commerce officials appear before Congress, or does a committee issue a subpoena, by 03 Dec? Assessment: Likely (moderate confidence), based on a reported 03 Dec summons and Huang's reported agreement to testify.
  2. Does Nvidia name a permanent CEO by 31 Dec? Assessment: Unlikely (low confidence), based on Kress serving as interim CEO with no permanent appointment announced.
  3. Do the Justice Department or FBI confirm an investigation of Nvidia or Huang by 31 Dec? Fox reports the Justice Department took over the Megaspeed probe; Megaspeed is a Nvidia partner, not Nvidia. Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on no confirmation naming Nvidia or Huang.
