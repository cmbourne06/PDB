Supreme Court Takes Up Trump’s January 2025 Order Restricting Asylum at the Southern Border

The Supreme Court agreed on 13 Nov to hear a challenge to Trump’s January 2025 order restricting asylum, Mullin v. Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services (No. 26-239). It will decide the order’s lawfulness and whether a judge may grant relief beyond the plaintiffs.

Key Facts

  • The Court limited review to Questions 1 and 2 of the petition, filed 24 Aug and conferenced 13 Nov; no argument date is set (Supreme Court docket).
  • A federal judge and a D.C. Circuit panel ruled against it; the panel said federal law gives the president discretion, not power to deny claims categorically (Roll Call).
  • The order stays in effect; both outlets report a decision by the end of the term in June or July 2027 (Roll Call; Epoch Times).

Points of Dispute

Lawfulness of the order. The administration says asylum is a discretionary benefit, the order is lawful, and border crossings have fallen (Epoch Times). The ACLU and RAICES say Congress set the asylum system by statute; the challengers’ brief says the president “may not invent by fiat a new removal system” (Roll Call). The Supreme Court has not ruled.

Watch

  1. Does the Court decide No. 26-239 before its term ends? Assessment: A decision by 31 Jul 2027 is very likely (moderate confidence), based on Roll Call and the Epoch Times reporting a June or July timeline; the docket shows no argument date.
  2. Is a funding bill enacted by 11 Dec without a lapse? A Hill headline reports GOP senators warning a Pentagon reconciliation push risks a shutdown. Assessment: A lapse after 11 Dec is unlikely (low confidence), based on the reported stopgap plan, weighed against that warning.

October Producer Prices Rise 0.3 Percent in the Month and 6.5 Percent Over 12 Months

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports October final-demand prices rose 0.3 percent on the month, seasonally adjusted, and 6.5 percent over 12 months, unadjusted. Goods prices rose 0.5 percent in the month and 10.2 percent over 12 months; the Federal Reserve meets on 08–09 Dec.

Change Since Last Report

The 13 Nov edition named this release as the next inflation input for W3.2.

Key Facts

  • Services prices rose 0.2 percent in the month; September’s rise was 1.4 percent, revised from 1.3 percent (BLS; Breitbart).
  • Breitbart, citing Econoday, says economists expected +0.4 percent and 6.5–6.6 percent, and calls the 12-month rate the largest since November 2022 (Breitbart).
  • Diesel fell 7.9 percent and unprocessed goods fell 3.1 percent as crude fell 10.3 percent (Breitbart).

Points of Dispute

Which core measure. BLS publishes final demand less foods, energy and trade: +0.5 percent in the month and +4.9 percent over 12 months (BLS). Breitbart reports final demand excluding food and energy: +0.3 percent and +5.0 percent (Breitbart). The two series exclude different categories, so the figures do not conflict.

FAA Lifts Nationwide Ground Stop After About Four Hours; Cause Under Review ↑ TRENDING

The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop at 3:54 pm ET on 13 Nov, and Transportation Secretary Duffy said airspace reopened at 8:15 pm ET. Duffy blames overloaded routers and switches; DHS Secretary Mullin sees no sign of a malicious attack, and the cause is under review.

Change Since Last Report

The 13 Nov on-demand report covered the stop through 5:23 pm ET. New: the lift, the cause statements and Duffy’s funding request.

Key Facts

  • Duffy said switches and routers “got overloaded with data” and gave inconsistent data, so the FAA shut the system down; he said it runs on analog telecom (Breitbart).
  • Duffy and Vought asked appropriators for $30.1 billion, $10.1 billion of it for air traffic control; Duffy now wants $50 billion (Roll Call; Al Jazeera).
  • FlightRadar24 reported over 4,700 cancellations and 10,000 delays on 13 Nov and about 700 cancellations for 14 Nov; Duffy says operations should be normal by Thanksgiving (Al Jazeera).

Points of Dispute

Cause. Duffy says router and switch overload. Al Jazeera says new technology overloaded antiquated parts, and former aviation officials ask why backups did not take over, citing a September GAO report (GAO-26-107992) on aging copper cabling (Al Jazeera). The FAA’s cause statement is not available.

Duration and rarity. Al Jazeera describes the stop as five hours in one report and more than four hours in another (Al Jazeera); the stated times give about 4 hours 20 minutes.

Watch

  1. Does the FAA name the failed system by 30 Nov? Assessment: A named system is likely (low confidence), based on Duffy already naming routers and switches; an FAA root-cause statement is not available.
  2. Do DHS or DOT report an AI system or intrusion contributed? The maker of the FAA’s SMART AI tool denies a link; Duffy confirms (Hill headline). Assessment: A finding that either contributed is unlikely (low confidence), based on Mullin’s statement and the denial.
  3. Does Congress fund air traffic control upgrades in a bill enacted by 11 Dec? Assessment: Funding by 11 Dec is unlikely (low confidence), based on no reported committee commitment and the 11 Dec funding dispute (W2.10).
