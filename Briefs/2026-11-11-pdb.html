Russia, Ukraine and US End First Day of Abu Dhabi Talks Without Agreement

Russia, Ukraine and the US held nearly four hours of talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 Nov, the first trilateral round in nine months, and no agreement was announced. Prisoner exchanges were discussed, Russia raised territory, and a second day is set for 11 Nov.

Change Since Last Report

The 09 Nov report covered Trump’s “different shape” territorial signal before the talks. Day one is now over without an announced agreement; the W1.4 assessment is unchanged.

Key Facts

  • Zelensky called the talks “constructive and quite substantive”; swaps of 1,000 or 1,500 prisoners per side were discussed, with technical maritime and energy ceasefire talks hoped for (Kyiv Independent).
  • TASS puts day one at about 4.5 hours including a 40-minute break, with only Russian, Ukrainian and US delegations in the room (TASS).
  • Fox News reports US officials want Russia to accept a Ukraine guarantee “effectively very similar to NATO”; Trump has not decided what the US would provide.
  • Rubio said on 09 Nov that any guarantee must also be acceptable to Russia, that no final plan exists, and that a treaty is not ruled out (Kyiv Independent).
  • Axios, as relayed by TASS, reports that Witkoff and Kushner may go to Moscow and then Kyiv after Abu Dhabi (TASS).

Points of Dispute

Territory. Russia raised territory on day one and says it holds half of Donbas; Zelensky says territory belongs at leaders’ level, and Kyiv disputes Moscow’s figure (The Kyiv Independent). US officials do not expect territory to be resolved in Abu Dhabi (Fox News).

Europe’s role. Russia insists the format is strictly trilateral, and Lavrov said Europeans could “sit in their hotel” (TASS). Europeans held separate coordination meetings with Ukraine (The Kyiv Independent).

Watch

  1. Day two, 11 Nov: does it produce a ceasefire framework or a leaders’ meeting? Assessment: Full terms agreed this round are unlikely (moderate confidence), based on open disputes over territory and no announced result from day one.
  2. Does Europe gain a seat in the room? Assessment: A formal European seat this round is very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on Russia’s insistence on a trilateral format.
  3. Is a prisoner exchange of 1,000 each or more announced by 30 Nov? Assessment: An announcement is roughly even chance (low confidence), based on swaps discussed but no agreement announced.

House Refers Hegseth Impeachment Articles to Committee, 211–204

The House voted 211–204 on 10 Nov to refer Rep. Thomas Massie’s eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, avoiding a direct vote on impeachment. Massie was the only Republican to oppose referral.

Change Since Last Report

The 10 Nov report said the House would vote by 11 Nov. It voted on 10 Nov to refer, not impeach, so W2.4 is resolved and W2.5, on Senate conviction, is moot.

Key Facts

  • All Democrats present opposed referral; eleven did not vote, including Pelosi. The Examiner gives no reasons; Al Jazeera’s live page cites Rep. Jonathan Jackson’s traffic delay.
  • The eight articles cover the Iran war, drug-boat strikes, retaliation against Sen. Mark Kelly and the capture of Maduro (Defense News; Fox News).
  • Republican leaders moved to refer the resolution instead of holding an impeachment vote, and Rep. Ellzey raised concern about Republican absences (Fox News).

Points of Dispute

Effect of referral. NPR’s headline says the vote effectively ends the impeachment effort (NPR). Defense News and the Washington Examiner say the referral leaves the articles in committee until Democrats take the House in January (Defense News; Washington Examiner).

Merits. Hegseth called the effort “a political spectacle” (Fox News) and a farce driven by ego and policy disagreement (Defense News). Rep. Bacon said he is no fan of Hegseth but rejects Massie’s articles, and Rep. Davidson called the Iran action unconstitutional but not unprecedented.

Assessment: A challenge in January is likely (moderate confidence), unchanged, based on Democratic oversight plans.

Economy, Business & Housing

October CPI Rises 0.4%; BLS Publishes No 12-Month Change

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October, above forecasts of 0.2 to 0.3 percent, with core prices up 0.3 percent. Fed Governor Christopher Waller supports a December rate hike and has opened the door to a half-point move.

Change Since Last Report

The 10 Nov report lacked the October result and gave 10 Dec for the FOMC decision; the Federal Reserve calendar shows 08–09 Dec. W3.4 is lowered because yields rose after the release.

Key Facts

  • BLS reports shelter rose 0.3 percent, energy 0.7 and food 0.5, seasonally adjusted. September’s all-items rise was 0.6 percent (BLS; HousingWire).
  • BLS published no 12-month changes because October 2025 data are missing after the appropriations lapse; HousingWire cites economists estimating annual inflation in the high 3 percent range (BLS; HousingWire).
  • Used cars fell 1.2 percent while motor vehicle insurance rose 0.7 and recreation 0.8 (HousingWire).
  • The 10-year Treasury yield rose 9 basis points about 25 minutes after the release; Freddie Mac’s prior survey put the 30-year mortgage rate at 7.18 percent (HousingWire).
  • AAA’s pump average fell to $3.98, the first reading below $4 since July (Al Jazeera), yet the seasonally adjusted gasoline index rose 1.0 percent after 9.1 in September (HousingWire).

Points of Dispute

Direction of inflation. Breitbart Business Digest’s Carney, in an opinion column, argues inflation is worsening and broadening beyond energy, and that the headline understates it; his category counts are his own calculation (Breitbart). BLS data show energy-driven moderation from September (BLS). NPR’s headline says inflation “stays stubbornly high” (NPR); NBC’s calls it “still a headache” (NBC News).

Watch

  1. Does the 08–09 Dec FOMC meeting raise rates after Waller’s support for a hike (The Hill)? Assessment: A hike is very likely (moderate confidence), based on Waller’s stated support and steady core inflation.
  2. Does the Freddie Mac 30-year rate fall a fourth week in the 12 Nov survey? Assessment: A fourth decline is unlikely (low confidence), based on the post-CPI rise in the 10-year Treasury yield.
