National Security & Foreign Affairs

Drone Attack at Kuwaiti Site Kills Two Italian Defense Workers

Two Italian defense workers died in an attack on a Kuwaiti site that Kuwait and the Gulf Cooperation Council attribute to Iran; Iran denies involvement. Italy is a U.S. treaty ally.

Change Since Last Report

The 26 Nov I&W item reported Iranian mine claims. UKMTO now describes the hits on two tankers as unknown projectiles, and the Kuwait attack killed two Italians.

Key Facts

  • Kuwait says debris killed the two Italians as it intercepted Iranian drones; a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani were wounded at a "vital site" (Al Jazeera).
  • Italy's foreign ministry says the dead were Rheinmetall Italia technicians at an air-defence surveillance station (Al Jazeera).
  • UKMTO says unknown projectiles hit two tankers near Hormuz, one Wednesday and one early Thursday, with crews safe (ABC Australia).
  • The Houthis struck Saudi facilities, including the Ras Tanura and Jazan refineries, within 24 hours (ABC Australia).
  • Trump told troops victory is "at hand" and named Pickaxe Mountain near Natanz as a target if strikes resume (Breitbart).

Points of Dispute

Attribution. Kuwait's defence ministry calls the attack "Iranian aggression." An Iranian military spokesperson denies any offensive operation and calls the accusation a "false flag" by Israel and the United States (Al Jazeera).

Tanker hits. The IRGC says the tankers struck mines. UKMTO reports unknown projectiles; AP could not verify either account (ABC Australia).

  2. Does the United States resume strikes on Iranian territory by 31 Jan 2027? Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on Trump naming a target and no U.S. strikes reported.
  3. Does the Pentagon or CENTCOM publicly attribute the Kuwait attack to Iran by 05 Dec? Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on Kuwaiti attribution and no U.S. statement available.

Topic 2  ·  PIR 2.1

Politics & Policy

Federal Judge Blocks FCC Early Review of ABC Station Licenses

A federal judge barred the FCC from reviewing eight ABC-owned station licenses early, finding the reviews were likely First Amendment retaliation. The ruling is a court check on an executive agency, and the FCC says it will keep using its authority.

Key Facts

  • Judge Loren AliKhan (D.D.C.) granted ABC and Disney a preliminary injunction on 25 Nov, writing "This is not a close case" (Epoch Times).
  • The FCC Media Bureau ordered the reviews in April, a day after Trump urged ABC to fire Jimmy Kimmel; the renewals were not due before 2028 (Epoch Times).
  • AliKhan denied ABC's broader request to block informal FCC coercion because ABC had not specified future threats (Epoch Times).
  • In a separate case AliKhan denied the DNC an injunction against about $20 million in DHS-funded ads, citing no irreparable harm after the midterms (Epoch Times).

Points of Dispute

Merits of the ruling. ABC calls the ruling a First Amendment victory. The FCC says the granted portion is "wrong on the facts and wrong on the law," and Justice Department lawyers had argued for dismissal (Epoch Times).

White House ads. The DNC calls the DHS-funded ads illegal government propaganda. The White House calls them public service announcements; AliKhan did not rule on the merits (Epoch Times).

Watch

  1. Does the FCC or the Justice Department appeal the AliKhan injunction by 15 Dec? Assessment: Likely (low confidence), based on the FCC's statement that the ruling is wrong on the law and that it will keep using its authority.
  2. Is a funding bill enacted by 11 Dec without a lapse? Assessment: A lapse is a roughly even chance (low confidence), based on the Thanksgiving recess and no floor action reported.
  3. Does a continuing resolution reach either chamber's floor by 05 Dec? Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on NewsNation's report that Republicans have days to act before Democrats take Congress.

Economy, Business & Housing

Trump Demands Investigation of Revised Third-Quarter GDP Figure

Trump asked for an immediate investigation of the Bureau of Economic Analysis's upward revision of third-quarter GDP, alleging a purposeful error. BEA's release credits investment and consumer spending; a BEA statement on the allegation is not available.

Change Since Last Report

The 26 Nov report carried the revised growth figure from one outlet; BEA's own release now confirms it. New: Trump's demand for a probe.

Key Facts

  • BEA, released 25 Nov: real GDP rose at a 4.3 percent annual rate in Q3 2026, revised from 3.4 percent in the advance estimate (BEA).
  • Software and intellectual-property investment led the revision; real GDI rose 4.4 percent and Q2 growth is revised to 2.2 percent (BEA).
  • The gross domestic purchases price index rose 2.7 percent, revised down 0.3 point; BEA's next release is 23 Dec (BEA).
  • Epoch Times notes BEA revised Q2 2020 GDP by 1.2 points, so large revisions have precedent (Epoch Times).
  • Freddie Mac's 30-year mortgage rate was 7.21 percent as of 25 Nov, down from 7.28 percent the prior week; no housing release fell on 26–27 Nov (Freddie Mac).

Points of Dispute

Cause of the revision. Trump says: "This is not a small error, this is a massive, and purposeful, error." Commerce Secretary Lutnick and NEC Director Hassett present the data as strong and do not endorse the claim of purpose (Epoch Times).

Watch

  1. Does the Commerce Department or BEA open a review of the Q3 revision or respond publicly to the demand by 15 Dec? Assessment: Unlikely (low confidence), based on Lutnick and Hassett presenting the data as strong.
  2. Does the 08–09 Dec FOMC raise rates? Assessment: Very likely (moderate confidence), based on October PCE inflation of 3.8 percent over 12 months and the output data above.
  3. Does the December FOMC raise the policy rate by 50 basis points rather than 25? Assessment: Unlikely (low confidence), based on core PCE rising 0.3 percent in October and market pricing centered on 25 basis points.

Topic 4  ·  PIR 4.1

Technology & Science

Reported Defense Production Act Talks on Nvidia's Board; Nvidia Pauses Shipments to Southeast Asia

The Washington Post reports, citing three people, that Hegseth and deputy Feinberg discussed using the Defense Production Act to pressure Nvidia's board over Jensen Huang. Separately, Nvidia reportedly told manufacturing partners to pause server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries.

Change Since Last Report

The 26 Nov report covered Senate Republicans questioning Huang's reinstatement. Coverage has since moved to Pentagon involvement and enforcement of chip export controls.

Key Facts

  • The Financial Times reports the pause covers systems bound for Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, and that Dell and Supermicro told customers (TASS).
  • On 23 Nov BIS blacklisted seven companies in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand; on 24 Nov Malaysia said it would advise data-center operators not to serve them (Epoch Times).
  • A BIS rule effective 19 Nov requires licenses for overseas data centers serving certain countries' firms; existing centers have 90 days (Epoch Times).
  • Sen. Cramer says Congress should codify the chip export order and expects it to pass in the NDAA, where the AI OVERWATCH Act is an amendment (Breitbart).
  • Attorney General Blanche declined to say whether DOJ investigated Huang before his reinstatement (Epoch Times).

Points of Dispute

Defense Production Act talks. The Post's three sources say the talks occurred. A Pentagon official says they never did; AEI's Bill Greenwalt says the law does not support such use (Daily Beast).

Huang's reinstatement. Nvidia says it drew on senior government input finding no national security risk, and Trump says Huang was exonerated. Democratic senators say export-control questions remain unanswered (Epoch Times).

Watch

  1. Do the Justice Department or FBI publicly confirm an investigation of Nvidia or Huang by 31 Dec? Assessment: Unlikely (low confidence), based on Blanche declining to say.
  2. Does either chamber advance a chip export-control bill by 31 Jan 2027? Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence; raised from unlikely), based on Cramer expecting the NDAA as the vehicle; no bill text reported.
  3. Does the Pentagon address the Post's Defense Production Act report on the record by 05 Dec? Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on one anonymous denial so far.
