Bahrain Says It Stopped Second Iranian Drone Attack; Third US Carrier Group Enters Region

Bahrain says its air defenses stopped Iranian drone attacks on 13 and 15 Nov, which the Revolutionary Guard claimed, while a US official says a third carrier group entered the Central Command area. The attacks bear on risk to US forces and Gulf allies; no damage assessment is available.

Change Since Last Report

The 15 Nov report carried Hormuz mine-strike headlines. New: the 15 Nov attack and Bahrain's statement, the carrier arrival, and the Guard's mine claim. W1.14 closes; W1.13 is unchanged.

Key Facts

  • Bahrain's Foreign Ministry said on 15 Nov it holds Iran “fully responsible” for both attacks (ABC News).
  • The USS Theodore Roosevelt joins the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington in the Arabian Sea; more than 20 US Navy ships enforce the blockade of Iranian ports (ABC News).
  • The Guard navy said an oil tanker hit naval mines east of Hormuz and caught fire at 21:55 UTC on 15 Nov; it provided no images (ABC News).
  • Bloomberg's 15 Nov headline says Iran struck a Bahrain base and repeated that the path to a US deal remains open (Bloomberg).

Points of Dispute

Effect of the attacks. The Guard claimed both attacks on Bahrain. Bahrain says its air defenses stopped them (ABC News). CENTCOM has not commented in the sources available.

Mine claim. The Guard navy says the tanker entered a restricted zone with its navigation off. No independent confirmation is available (ABC News).

  2. Does CENTCOM or Bahrain confirm damage from the Guard's 13 Nov radar-strike claim by 20 Nov? Assessment: Very unlikely (low confidence), based on Bahrain's statement that its defenses stopped the attacks and no CENTCOM confirmation.
  3. Does CENTCOM or UKMTO attribute the 16 Nov tanker strike near Hormuz, reported after this window, to Iran by 23 Nov (ABC News)? Assessment: Roughly even chance (low confidence), based on UKMTO reporting an unknown projectile.

FAA Investigation Continues Into Nationwide Ground Stop; L3Harris Pulls Routers

The cause of the 13 Nov nationwide ground stop remains under an FAA-led investigation, and L3Harris has pulled routers that may have caused it, NewsNation reports. Officials have said they do not believe the stop was a cyberattack.

Change Since Last Report

The 15 Nov report covered officials' attribution to router overload. New: NewsNation reports the routers were pulled for investigation. No FAA root cause is available; funding is under Topic 2.

Key Facts

  • Duffy said L3Harris was replacing copper wiring with fiber when routers overloaded and gave bad data; officials are checking contractor error against a faulty router (Al Jazeera; Epoch Times).
  • FAA Administrator Bedford said there is no evidence of anything nefarious or AI-related (Al Jazeera; Epoch Times).
  • The stop began 3:54 pm ET on 13 Nov, and airspace reopened at 8:15 pm ET (Epoch Times).

Points of Dispute

Whether a cyber intrusion contributed. Duffy said “we don't believe this was cyber at all.” Mullin saw no sign of a cyberattack but did not rule out other causes (Al Jazeera). An FAA root-cause statement is not available.

Watch

  1. Does the FAA name the failed system by 30 Nov? Assessment: Likely (low confidence), based on Duffy's attribution to routers and an FAA-led investigation; no FAA root-cause statement is available.
  2. Do DHS or DOT report that an AI system or cyber intrusion contributed? Assessment: Very unlikely (moderate confidence), based on Duffy's and Bedford's statements across color groups; Mullin's hedge keeps it above almost no chance.
