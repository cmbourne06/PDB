Abu Dhabi Talks End With Prisoner Exchange Agreed, No Breakthrough; US Envoys Go to Moscow

The Abu Dhabi talks ended on 11 Nov with Russia’s chief negotiator citing a “fundamental agreement” on another large prisoner exchange and, per CNN’s headline, no breakthrough. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff go to Moscow on 12 Nov.

Change Since Last Report

The 11 Nov report covered day one, which ended without agreement. Day two produced the exchange agreement and the Moscow trip. W1.4 closes with no ceasefire framework or leaders’ meeting reported, as assessed.

Key Facts

  • Ukrainian negotiator Rustem Umerov named the exchange agreement as the main result; neither side has given numbers or a date (The Kyiv Independent).
  • Kushner said, “We see some openings and some opportunities”; the envoys then brief Zelensky at the 13 Nov informal EU leaders’ meeting in Dublin (The Kyiv Independent).
  • Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov said Putin would meet the envoys soon, and Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said Putin will decide Russia’s next steps after their briefing (TASS).
  • NBC News’s headline also reports a large prisoner exchange agreed and no major breakthrough (NBC News).

Points of Dispute

Size of the exchange. Russian state media, citing Gen. Alexander Zorin, say it would be the largest ever, with over 1,000 on each side (TASS). Ukraine has given no numbers; Zelensky said on 10 Nov that swaps of 1,000 or 1,500 per side had been discussed (The Kyiv Independent).

Outcome. Both delegations point to the exchange as the result; NBC News and CNN headlines say no major breakthrough.

Watch

  1. Is an exchange of 1,000 each or more announced by 30 Nov? Assessment: An announcement is likely (low confidence), based on the agreement in principle and a Russian state-media figure above 1,000 that Ukraine has not given.
  2. Does Putin receive Kushner and Witkoff in Moscow, and does Russia state its next steps? Assessment: A Putin meeting by 15 Nov is very likely (moderate confidence), based on Ushakov’s statement that Putin would meet them soon.
  3. Do Kushner and Witkoff brief Zelensky in Dublin on 13 Nov? Assessment: The briefing is very likely (moderate confidence), based on the scheduled stop reported after the talks.

Core CPI Reaches Waller’s Threshold for Considering a 50-Basis-Point Hike; Mortgage Rates Jump

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports core consumer prices rose 0.3 percent in October, the level at which Fed Governor Christopher Waller said on 10 Nov that a 50-basis-point December hike should be considered. CNBC’s headline reports mortgage rates jumped on 11 Nov after four straight weeks of easing.

Change Since Last Report

The 11 Nov report relied on headlines for Waller’s position. His speech text, now read, sets the 50-basis-point condition above; W3.4 is lowered and W3.5 is new.

Key Facts

  • Waller spoke before the release and said he leans toward supporting a 25-basis-point hike in December if October inflation improved as he expected (Federal Reserve).
  • He supported holding in October because markets had priced in tightening, and expects further tightening “sooner rather than later” (Federal Reserve).
  • He named AI-related high-tech goods prices and elevated energy prices as upside risks to inflation (Federal Reserve).
  • Waller described the labor market as stable and near maximum employment, with low hiring and low firing, and raised concern about longer unemployment spells (Federal Reserve).
  • BLS published no 12-month change because October 2025 data were not collected during the appropriations lapse (BLS).

Points of Dispute

Size of the December hike. Waller would consider the larger move only if core CPI met his stated threshold (Federal Reserve). The Washington Post’s headline says he signals a “big rate hike” (Washington Post); CNBC’s says he expects a December hike and opens the door to a half-point move (CNBC).

Mortgage-rate series. CNBC’s headline cites four straight weeks of easing; Freddie Mac’s survey fell three weeks to 7.18 percent (HousingWire). CNBC’s series is not identified.

Watch

  1. Does the 08–09 Dec FOMC meeting raise rates? Assessment: A hike is very likely (moderate confidence), based on Waller’s stated lean toward a hike and the BLS core CPI reading.
  2. Does the Freddie Mac 30-year rate fall a fourth week in the survey due 12 Nov at 12:00 ET? Assessment: A fourth decline is very unlikely (low confidence), based on CNBC’s headline on 11 Nov and the post-CPI rise in the 10-year yield.
  3. Does the December FOMC raise the policy rate by 50 basis points rather than 25? Assessment: A 50-basis-point hike is unlikely (low confidence), based on Waller’s conditional wording and no other FOMC member’s stated position in the sources reviewed.
