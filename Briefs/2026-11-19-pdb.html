Iran Claims Drone Strike on U.S. Base in Kuwait as Hormuz Tanker Is Hit

Iran’s army says drones targeted U.S. installations at Ali Al Salem air base in Kuwait; the Pentagon has not responded and no damage was reported. A UAE-flagged ADNOC vessel was struck in Hormuz on 17 Nov, injuring 11 crew.

Change Since Last Report

The 18 Nov report covered Bahrain, the Qeshm strike and Kuwait drones. New: the Ali Al Salem claim, casualties on the ADNOC vessel, Gulf condemnations and a dispute over Hormuz insurance costs.

Key Facts

  • Bahrain says its air defenses intercepted Iranian attacks; no casualties or damage were reported in Kuwait or Bahrain (Epoch Times; Times of Israel).
  • The ADNOC vessel struck on the evening of 17 Nov left 11 crew injured and one missing; UKMTO cited an unknown projectile and did not name the ship (Epoch Times; Times of Israel).
  • Qatar, the UAE and Saudi Arabia condemned the Iranian attacks, and Saudi Arabia also condemned the tanker attack (Epoch Times).
  • Acting Navy Secretary Cao told senators one Hormuz transit now carries $64 million in insurance; analysts called the figure unverified, citing $10–21 million elsewhere, and said traffic is about 65 percent below pre-war levels (Al Jazeera).
  • CENTCOM said the Bahrain-bound drones were intercepted without damage or injury and that Iran has recently targeted U.S. assets in Bahrain (Fox News).

Points of Dispute

Kuwait strike claim. Iran’s army says drones targeted U.S. drone and missile installations at Ali Al Salem. The Epoch Times could not verify it and the Pentagon did not respond; Kuwait called it a violation of its sovereignty (Epoch Times).

Hormuz status. President Trump says the campaign is effectively over and the Navy escorts ships. Iran says it controls passage and requires approved routes (Al Jazeera).

  2. Does CENTCOM or UKMTO attribute the 16 Nov tanker strike to Iran by 23 Nov? Assessment: roughly even chance (low confidence), based on UKMTO citing an unknown projectile in both strikes.
  3. Does the Pentagon or Kuwait confirm damage at Ali Al Salem by 26 Nov? Assessment: very unlikely (low confidence), based on no reported damage and CENTCOM’s earlier denials.

Administration Asks Supreme Court a Third Time to Allow End of Venezuelan TPS

The Justice Department asked the Supreme Court on 18 Nov to pause a district court ruling that officials lacked authority to end Temporary Protected Status for Venezuelans. The ruling is due to take effect on 20 Nov unless a court suspends it.

Key Facts

  • Judge Edward Chen ruled that officials lacked authority to end Venezuelan TPS; the Supreme Court paused an earlier Chen ruling in October 2025 (Al Jazeera).
  • The administration says reinstatement blocks up to 332 removal orders and could release about 1,800 detainees (Al Jazeera).
  • Plaintiffs say TPS holders have since been detained, separated from their children and deported (Al Jazeera).

Points of Dispute

Whether the ruling is paused. Al Jazeera reports it takes effect on 20 Nov unless a federal appeals court or the Supreme Court suspends it. The Hill’s headline says an appeals court paused it, easing the need for Supreme Court action (The Hill, headline only). The conflict is unresolved; no docket entry is available.

Harm from reinstatement. The administration cites “severe and immediate harm” and 332 removal orders. Plaintiffs cite detention, family separation and deportation of TPS holders (Al Jazeera).

Watch

  1. Does the Supreme Court grant the stay by 30 Nov? Assessment: likely (low confidence), based on the Court’s October 2025 pause of an earlier Chen ruling.
  2. Does the ruling take effect on 20 Nov, or does a court suspend it first? Assessment: roughly even chance (low confidence), based on conflicting reports of an appeals court pause.

Commerce Remote-Access Chip Rule Reaches Federal Register; House Panel Presses Nvidia and Lutnick

Commerce’s interim final rule on remote access to advanced semiconductors is scheduled for Federal Register publication on 19 Nov and adds license requirements for five countries. House Energy and Commerce Chair Guthrie asked Nvidia’s interim chief executive and Secretary Lutnick to appear.

Change Since Last Report

The 18 Nov report covered the rule’s announcement, the Bessent–Lutnick split and Huang testimony. New: the Federal Register text, its five-country license reach and House committee requests.

Key Facts

  • The rule (document 2026-23529, docket BIS-2026-0628) takes effect on publication, and comments are due 60 days later (Federal Register public inspection).
  • It controls remote access to advanced chips through overseas data centers serving firms headquartered in Macau or Country Group D:5 (Federal Register).
  • It adds license requirements for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule (Federal Register).
  • Huang and Nvidia’s general counsel are set for closed-door House interviews on 1–2 Dec; Sen. Ricketts introduced the MEGA Act on Megaspeed (Fox News).
  • Guthrie asked Nvidia’s interim CEO and Lutnick to appear, and Pence said Lutnick “may need to go” (The Hill; The Hill, headlines only).

Points of Dispute

Hearing format and date. Fox reports closed-door House interviews on 1–2 Dec and a public Senate Banking hearing with no date. Al Jazeera reported a 3 Dec Senate summons on 17–18 Nov (link not available this edition). No committee notice is available to settle it.

Watch

  1. Do Huang or Commerce officials appear before Congress, or does a committee issue a subpoena, by 03 Dec? Assessment: likely (moderate confidence), based on the scheduled 1–2 Dec House interviews.
  2. Do the Justice Department or FBI confirm an investigation of Nvidia or Huang by 31 Dec? Assessment: roughly even chance (low confidence), based on the Justice Department’s reported Megaspeed probe and an SEC inquiry that does not count.
