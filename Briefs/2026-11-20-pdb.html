Trump, Xi and Putin Adopt Four-Point Declaration at Shenzhen Summit

The three leaders met about 90 minutes in Shenzhen on 19 Nov and adopted a four-point declaration on UN reform, energy supply, international law and nuclear security. The declaration text is not available, and the reports read describe no Ukraine or Iran commitment.

Change Since Last Report

The 18 and 19 Nov I&W items previewed the meeting. New: it took place and produced a signed declaration, so W1.15 closes and W1.16 is met, against the 14 Nov assessment of unlikely.

Key Facts

  • The declaration reaffirms stable energy flows, pledges talks on lowering nuclear risks and criticizes the International Criminal Court, to which none of the three states belongs (Al Jazeera).
  • Trump told reporters, “We are really making history” (Kyiv Independent). No joint remarks followed the meeting (TASS).
  • Trump invited Putin to the G20 in Miami next month; Putin has skipped recent G20 summits because of an ICC warrant (Al Jazeera).
  • Trump later said nuclear arms limits with China and Russia are very hard to achieve and that he will keep pushing (Epoch Times, headline and description).

Points of Dispute

Agenda. The White House listed the Iran war on the agenda. Al Jazeera reports it went unmentioned except for Trump saying Tehran cannot hold a nuclear weapon (Al Jazeera).

Ukraine’s role. The Kremlin says Ukraine was not the central trilateral topic but dominated the Putin–Trump bilateral. Ukraine’s foreign minister said a trilateral without Kyiv encourages Putin to continue the war (Kyiv Independent).

Watch

  2. Does Putin attend the G20 in Miami next month? Assessment: very unlikely (low confidence), based on his skipping recent G20 summits over the ICC warrant.

The 13 Nov report covered Democrats weighing Iran limits on the deadline. New: four named senators commit to opposing a stopgap without them, and the Senate rejected the war-powers resolution.

Key Facts

  • Duckworth, Bennet, Sanders and Van Hollen each said they will vote against any short-term package that does not limit the Iran conflict (Washington Examiner).
  • Top House appropriators are trying to avoid a holiday-season shutdown (Washington Examiner).
  • The Senate voted 48–51 against the resolution directing an end to the Iran war; Collins, Murkowski and Paul voted yes, and Tillis, who had backed it before, voted no (Epoch Times).

Mortgage Rate Rises to 7.28 Percent as Multifamily Starts Jump and Permits Fall

Freddie Mac’s 30-year fixed rate rose to 7.28 percent on 19 Nov from 7.11 percent, ending four weekly declines. October multifamily starts jumped while permits fell, and analysts dispute whether the starts surge is real.

Change Since Last Report

The 18 Nov report covered NAHB builder confidence of 25. New: the rate rose, so W3.6 closes, because a fifth weekly decline did not occur, as assessed.

Key Facts

  • The 30-year rate was 6.26 percent a year earlier; the 15-year rate was 6.66 percent (Freddie Mac).
  • Census put October total starts at 1,309,000, up 9.9 percent (±16.8 percent), and permits at 1,295,000, down 7.4 percent (Census Bureau, 18 Nov release).
  • In buildings of five or more units, October starts ran at 433,000 and permits at 475,000 (Census Bureau).
  • HousingWire reports multifamily starts rose 25.5 percent while permits fell 5.2 percent; the Census page read gives no percent change for these series (HousingWire).
  • Cushman & Wakefield reports construction pipelines shrank year over year in more than 70 percent of 90 markets (HousingWire).

Points of Dispute

Whether the multifamily surge is real. Analyst Jay Parsons says the federal starts figures do not square with builder and private data. Cushman & Wakefield says “The pullback in construction activity is broad-based.” The Census margin of ±16.8 percent applies to total starts; a Census response is not available (HousingWire).

Watch

  1. Does Freddie Mac’s 30-year rate in the next weekly survey exceed 7.28 percent? Assessment: roughly even chance (low confidence), based on one week of data after four declines.
  2. Does the 8–9 Dec FOMC raise rates? Assessment: very likely (moderate confidence), based on the 19 Nov minutes and futures pricing.
